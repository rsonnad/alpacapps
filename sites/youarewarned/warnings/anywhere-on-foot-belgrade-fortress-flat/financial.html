2. The refund that was agreed

On 10 August, after the condition of the apartment was raised, the host offered a refund — conditional on her own staff's inspection:

"At this point, the best thing I can do is issue you a refund once you check out of the apartment tomorrow, provided that my cleaning team confirms that there has been no damage or misuse of the apartment."

Host, 10 Aug 15:25

Asked how much, she quoted one night: €44.48. Then, unprompted:

"Of course I can offer more, 2% more. I will have to Mark you as a no-show on booking so I don't have to pay their commission that way you can get a bigger refund. If that's what you want let me know"

Host, 10 Aug 15:35

I did not agree to being recorded as a no-show.

4. The position as it stands

The booking ran to 11 August and I checked out on 11 August, when I removed my belongings from the apartment. On the afternoon of 10 August, because of the condition of the flat, I paid for a room elsewhere and slept there — but I had not checked out. My belongings were still in the apartment, I still held the keys, and the apartment was still let to me.

The host put the correct checkout date in writing herself — the day before she changed it

On 10 August at 15:05 she wrote:

"As your scheduled checkout is tomorrow, you are welcome to continue using the apartment until your checkout time."

And at 15:25:

"the best thing I can do is issue you a refund once you check out of the apartment tomorrow"

"Tomorrow" was 11 August. On 11 August she amended the record to show departure on 10 August — a date she had twice stated in writing, twenty-four hours earlier, was not my checkout date.

And the refund the amendment was supposedly executing was never paid

Shortening a booking is the ordinary mechanism a host uses to process a partial refund. Here the night was removed from the record — relieving the host of Booking.com's commission on it and the residence tax on it, which is precisely the outcome she had described in writing the day before — and no refund has been paid. Both sides of the transaction ended up with her.
