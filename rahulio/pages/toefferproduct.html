AI Foundations for Product Managers

What You'll Learn

Generative AI refers to systems that create new content — text, code, images, data analysis — based on patterns learned from vast amounts of existing data. Unlike traditional software that follows explicit rules ("if X then Y"), generative AI models predict what comes next based on context you provide.

Key Concepts

  • Large Language Models (LLMs) — The engines behind tools like Claude and ChatGPT. They process your text input (called a "prompt") and generate relevant output. Think of them as extremely well-read assistants that can synthesize information.
  • Prompts — The instructions you give to an AI tool. The quality of your output depends heavily on the clarity and specificity of your prompt. This is the single most important skill you'll develop.
  • Tokens — How AI models measure text length. Roughly 1 token ≈ ¾ of a word. This matters because models have a "context window" (how much text they can consider at once).
  • Hallucinations — When an AI generates plausible-sounding but incorrect information. In financial services, this is a critical risk to manage. Always verify AI outputs against authoritative sources.
Financial Services Context: In your world, AI isn't replacing product managers — it's amplifying what you can do. You'll move faster on market research, vendor evaluations, spec writing, and compliance documentation while keeping human judgment on final decisions.

Why This Matters First

Before you start using AI tools in financial services, you must understand the guardrails. Financial services is one of the most regulated industries, and misusing AI can create serious legal exposure.

Rules to Live By

  • Never paste customer PII into public AI tools. Names, account numbers, SSNs, addresses — none of it goes into Claude, ChatGPT, or any external AI tool unless your company has an enterprise agreement with data protections in place.
  • Check your company's AI policy. Most financial institutions now have one. If yours doesn't, flag it — you'll be ahead of the curve.
  • Enterprise vs. consumer tiers matter. Enterprise plans (Claude for Enterprise, ChatGPT Enterprise, Copilot for M365) typically include contractual guarantees that your data won't be used to train models. Consumer/free tiers usually do not.
  • Audit trail thinking. If a regulator asks "how did you arrive at this decision?" you need to be able to explain it. AI-assisted ≠ AI-decided.
Practical tip: Create a personal "safe/not-safe" list. Safe: publicly available market data, your own draft text, hypothetical scenarios. Not safe: anything with real customer data, internal financial figures, proprietary trading strategies, or unreleased product details.

The Prompt Framework: CRAFT

Use this framework every time you interact with an AI tool:

  • C — Context: Tell the AI who you are and what situation you're in. "I'm a product manager at a mid-size bank evaluating payment processing vendors."
  • R — Role: Tell the AI who it should be. "Act as a senior fintech analyst."
  • A — Ask: State exactly what you want. "Compare Stripe, Adyen, and Marqeta across these 5 dimensions..."
  • F — Format: Specify how you want the output. "Present this as a comparison table with pros/cons."
  • T — Tone: Set the communication style. "Write this for a C-suite audience — concise, no jargon."

Example: Bad vs. Good Prompt

Bad: Tell me about payment APIs

Good: I'm a PM at a regional bank exploring whether to integrate Plaid's transaction API for our small business customers. Summarize Plaid's key capabilities, pricing model, compliance certifications (SOC 2, PCI), and the top 3 risks I should raise with our compliance team. Format as bullet points.

Deep Dive: Claude for Product Work

~90 min total

Why Claude Stands Out for PMs

Claude can process extremely long documents — up to 200K tokens (~150K words, or a 500-page document) in a single conversation. This is a game-changer for product managers who deal with:

  • Vendor contracts & SOWs — Upload the full document and ask Claude to extract key terms, SLAs, liability clauses, and pricing structures.
  • API documentation — Feed it a vendor's API docs and ask "What data can we pull from this API for our small business dashboard?"
  • Regulatory filings — Summarize lengthy compliance documents into actionable requirements for your product team.
  • Competitive analysis — Upload multiple competitors' public materials and ask for a structured comparison.

How to Upload Documents

On claude.ai, click the paperclip icon to attach files (PDF, DOCX, TXT, CSV). Then reference the document in your prompt: Based on the attached vendor agreement, list all SLA commitments and their penalty clauses.

Data safety reminder: Only upload documents that are appropriate per your company's AI usage policy. When in doubt, redact customer data first or use hypothetical examples.

The Workflow

Instead of starting from a blank page, use Claude as a collaborative drafting partner:

  1. Brain dump first. Type out everything you know about the feature in rough notes — don't worry about structure.
  2. Ask Claude to structure it. Turn these rough notes into a PRD following this template: [Problem Statement, User Stories, Requirements, Success Metrics, Risks, Timeline].
  3. Iterate. Ask Claude to strengthen weak sections: The success metrics section is too vague. Suggest 3 specific, measurable KPIs for a payment integration feature at a bank.
  4. Challenge it. What am I missing? What edge cases or failure modes should I add to the requirements?

Example Prompt for 3rd-Party Integration PRD

I'm writing a PRD for integrating [Vendor X]'s identity verification API into our customer onboarding flow. Our users are small business owners applying for business checking accounts. Draft a PRD that covers: the problem we're solving, integration requirements, data flow (what we send to the vendor, what we get back), compliance considerations for KYC/AML, success metrics, and rollback plan if the integration fails. Keep it under 2 pages.

Pro tip: Save your best prompts in a personal "prompt library" doc. Over time, you'll build reusable templates for common PM documents.

Projects (Claude Pro/Team Feature)

Claude's Projects feature lets you create a persistent workspace with pre-loaded context. This is extremely useful for ongoing product work:

  • Create a project per initiative. E.g., "Plaid Integration Q3" or "Vendor Evaluation — KYC Providers."
  • Add reference documents to the project's knowledge base. Claude will reference them in every conversation within that project.
  • Set custom instructions (called "Project Instructions") that tell Claude your role, your company's context, and how you want responses formatted.

Sample Project Instructions

You are helping a product manager at a mid-size US bank. Our customers are small businesses (1-50 employees). We operate under OCC and state banking regulations. When suggesting integrations, always flag compliance implications. Format responses with headers and bullet points. Be specific about technical requirements but explain them in business terms.

Conversation Best Practices

  • Start new conversations for new topics — don't mix vendor evaluation with sprint planning in one thread.
  • Use follow-up messages to refine, not restart. Claude remembers everything in the current conversation.
  • When a conversation gets long, ask Claude to Summarize our key decisions and open questions so far before continuing.

Why PMs Need to Understand APIs

When your job involves integrating third-party products, you don't need to write code — but you need to understand what's possible, how data flows, and where the risks are. Claude can be your API translator.

Practical Exercises

  • Decode API docs: Here is the Plaid Transactions API documentation. Explain in plain English: what data can we get, what do we need to send, and what are the rate limits? I'm a PM, not an engineer.
  • Map data flows: Draw me an ASCII diagram showing how data flows between our app, Plaid's API, and the customer's bank. Include what happens when the connection fails.
  • Spot integration risks: Based on this API documentation, what are the top 5 things that could go wrong during integration? For each risk, suggest a mitigation we should include in our requirements.
  • Translate for stakeholders: Our engineering team sent me this technical spec for the Vendor X integration. Rewrite the key points for our VP of Product — focus on business impact, timeline risks, and resource needs.
Antigravity tip: If you're using Antigravity alongside Claude, use Antigravity to manage the overall integration initiative (tracking, prioritization, stakeholder updates) and Claude for deep-dive analysis on specific technical questions.

The Task

Choose a publicly available fintech API (Plaid, Stripe, Marqeta, Alloy, Sardine — any that interests you). Use Claude to:

  1. Summarize the API's core capabilities in PM-friendly language
  2. Create a data flow diagram (ask for ASCII or Mermaid format)
  3. List 5 questions you'd ask the vendor before committing

Advanced Prompting & Workflow Automation

Beyond Basic Prompting

Now that you're comfortable with CRAFT prompts, here are two powerful techniques that dramatically improve AI output quality:

Chain of Thought (CoT)

Ask the AI to show its reasoning step by step before giving a final answer. This is especially valuable for complex financial decisions.

We're deciding whether to build our own fraud detection system or integrate a third-party solution. Think through this step by step: consider build cost, time to market, ongoing maintenance, compliance implications, competitive advantage, and total cost of ownership over 3 years. Then give your recommendation.

Why it works: When AI shows its work, it catches more edge cases and produces more nuanced analysis. It also makes the reasoning auditable — critical in financial services.

Few-Shot Prompting

Show the AI examples of what good output looks like before asking for new output.

Here's how I write user stories for our team: [paste 2-3 real examples]. Now write 5 user stories for our new [feature] following the same format, level of detail, and acceptance criteria style.

Why it works: Instead of describing your format in abstract terms, you show the AI exactly what you want. The output will closely match your examples' style and depth.

Combining techniques: You can use CRAFT + CoT + Few-Shot together. Give context and role (CRAFT), show examples (Few-Shot), and ask for step-by-step reasoning (CoT). This triple-stack produces the highest quality output.

Your Personal Prompt Library

After a few weeks of AI use, you'll notice you're doing the same types of tasks repeatedly. Build templates for these — it's one of the highest-ROI activities for an AI-powered PM.

Templates to Build

  • Integration PRD Kickoff: Draft a PRD outline for integrating [Vendor API] into [our product area]. Include: problem statement, user impact, technical requirements (what data flows where), compliance requirements for [regulation], success metrics, and risks.
  • Sprint Review Prep: Here are the tickets we completed this sprint: [list]. Generate: (1) A demo script for stakeholders (2) Key metrics to highlight (3) Any items that need follow-up next sprint.
  • Customer Impact Assessment: We're adding [feature/integration]. For our [customer segment], describe: (1) What changes for them (2) Migration steps if any (3) FAQ they'll likely ask (4) Draft customer communication.
Where to store templates: A simple Google Doc, Notion page, or even a Claude Project with your templates in the Project Instructions. The key is having them findable when you need them.

AI for Integration Planning & Execution

~90 min total

Why This Matters

Third-party integrations in financial services are rarely simple. They involve multiple teams (engineering, compliance, legal, ops), external dependencies (vendor timelines, certifications), and regulatory gates. AI can help you map and manage this complexity.

Dependency Mapping Prompt

I'm planning the integration of [Vendor]'s [product] into our [system]. Help me create a dependency map. For each workstream below, list: prerequisites, team responsible, estimated duration, and what blocks/is blocked by other workstreams. Workstreams: (1) Legal/contract review (2) Compliance/security assessment (3) API integration development (4) Testing & QA (5) Customer migration (6) Internal training. Present as a table and then identify the critical path.

Using AI for Timeline Planning

Given these dependencies [paste your map], create a realistic integration timeline. Flag any risks where parallel workstreams could conflict. Assume: legal review takes 4-6 weeks in financial services, compliance assessment needs SOC 2 Type II report review, and we have 2 backend engineers available.

Reality check: AI timelines are optimistic baselines. In financial services, add buffer for compliance review cycles, legal back-and-forth, and vendor responsiveness. A good rule: take the AI estimate and add 30-50% for regulated environment friction.

Making Compliance Work Faster, Not Shortcuts

AI doesn't replace your compliance team — but it dramatically reduces the time you spend preparing materials for them.

Pre-Compliance Checklist Generation

We're integrating a third-party [KYC/payments/data] provider at a US bank. Generate a pre-integration compliance checklist that covers: data handling (where customer data goes, encryption, retention), regulatory requirements (BSA/AML, GLBA, state regulations), vendor risk management (FFIEC guidance), business continuity (what happens if vendor goes down), and audit requirements. Format as a checklist I can share with our compliance team.

Risk Register Building

For our [Vendor] integration, create a risk register with columns: Risk ID, Description, Category (Technical/Compliance/Operational/Reputational), Likelihood (High/Medium/Low), Impact (High/Medium/Low), Mitigation Strategy, Owner, Status. Generate 10-15 risks specific to a [category] integration in banking.

Compliance Q&A Prep

Our compliance team will review this integration proposal next week. Based on the integration plan I've described, what are the top 10 questions they're likely to ask? For each question, draft a prepared response I can customize.

The AI-Assisted Business Case

Use Claude to build financially rigorous business cases for integration investments:

Help me build a business case for integrating [Vendor]'s [product]. Here are my inputs: Implementation cost: $[X] (engineering time) + $[Y] (vendor fees). Expected benefits: [describe — faster onboarding, reduced fraud, new revenue stream]. Customer base affected: [N] business customers. Build a 3-year TCO model, ROI calculation, and break-even analysis. Include a sensitivity analysis: what happens if adoption is 50% of forecast? Present in a format suitable for a CFO review.

Key Business Case Components AI Can Draft

  • Executive summary — The "why now" in 3 sentences
  • Market context — Competitive pressure, customer demand signals
  • Financial model — Costs, revenue impact, ROI, payback period
  • Risk/mitigation — What could go wrong and our plan
  • Go-to-market — How customers will adopt this
  • Resource ask — What you need (people, budget, timeline)
Important: AI can structure the business case and do the math, but the inputs (costs, expected adoption rates, revenue projections) need to come from you and your stakeholders. Garbage in = garbage out, even with AI.

Analyzing Customer Feedback at Scale

When deciding which integrations to prioritize, customer data is gold. AI helps you process it faster:

Techniques

  • Feedback categorization: Here are 50 customer support tickets mentioning payment issues [paste or upload]. Categorize each by: root cause, severity, frequency, and which of our current vendors is involved. Identify the top 3 patterns.
  • Interview synthesis: I conducted 8 customer interviews about their needs for [feature area]. Here are my raw notes [paste]. Synthesize into: top themes (with frequency), direct quotes that illustrate each theme, and surprising insights I might have missed.
  • Survey analysis: Analyze these NPS survey responses [paste]. Group by sentiment, identify the top 5 drivers of negative scores, and suggest 3 product improvements that could move the needle.
  • Feature request prioritization: Here are 30 feature requests from our business banking customers [paste]. Score each by: frequency of request, revenue impact (based on requesting customer size), alignment with our strategy, and implementation complexity. Recommend the top 5 to build next quarter.
Data privacy: When analyzing customer feedback, make sure to anonymize or redact any personally identifiable information before pasting into AI tools. Replace names with "Customer A/B/C" and remove account numbers.

Mistakes New AI Users Make

  • Over-trusting AI output. AI sounds confident even when wrong. Always verify facts, especially numbers, dates, vendor claims, and regulatory details. Treat AI output as a first draft from a smart intern — helpful but needs review.
  • Under-specifying prompts. "Write me a PRD" gives generic output. "Write me a PRD for [specific feature] at [specific type of company] for [specific customer segment]" gives useful output. Specificity is everything.
  • Ignoring data privacy. In the excitement of a new tool, it's easy to paste sensitive data. Build the habit of scanning your prompt for PII before hitting enter.
  • Trying to automate judgment. AI can analyze options and present tradeoffs. It should not make the final call on strategic decisions, vendor selection, or customer-impacting changes. That's your job.
  • Not iterating. Your first prompt rarely gets the best output. Think of AI conversations as collaborative — refine, push back, ask for alternatives. "That's too generic, make it more specific to mid-market banks" is a perfectly valid follow-up.
  • Tool hopping. Pick 2-3 tools and go deep rather than surface-level with 7 tools. Mastery of Claude alone will cover 80% of your AI-assisted PM needs.
