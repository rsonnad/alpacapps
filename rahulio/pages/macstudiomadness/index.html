The appreciation is real — and temporary

A supply shock, not a durable trend. Three things happened in six months.

Estimated value of a 512 GB M3 Ultra Mac Studio

Supply shock

Read the dashed bar with suspicion. Listings at $25,500–$37,000 are asking prices on a thin market, and eBay asks for scarce hardware are frequently fantasy. Sold-listing data could not be found — that is the number that matters. Check eBay's completed/sold filter and r/hardwareswap before anchoring on anything here.

What happened

  • March 2026 — Apple silently pulls the 512 GB option; raises the 256 GB upgrade by $400.
  • May 2026 — 128 GB and 256 GB pulled too.
  • Today — max orderable config is 96 GB.

You cannot buy 512 GB of unified memory in a Mac at any price. That is the entire basis of the premium.

Why it won't last

The premium is a DRAM supply constraint, not a property of the silicon. When supply normalizes and an M5 Ultra ships, this becomes a two-generation-old machine whose only distinction was a memory tier the new one also offers.

The window to sell into scarcity is now, not in two years.

What the machine actually produces

The best case for keeping it is running a frontier open model locally. Here is what that's worth against simply buying the same tokens.

The local machine competes with the cheap row, not the expensive one. It runs DeepSeek-class models — so the honest benchmark is DeepSeek's own cloud price, which is under a dollar per million output tokens. It does not produce Opus-quality output, so the $25 row isn't a substitution it can make.

The core problem

A machine that generates 17 tokens per second is producing something that costs $0.87 per million to buy. Running flat out for a full day yields about 1.47M tokens — roughly $1.28 of substitutable value, against about $0.96 of electricity.

And scenario 2 cannot actually be run

There is no marketplace for Apple Silicon compute. Vast.ai, RunPod, Akash, and TensorDock are CUDA-only — their entire tooling stack targets NVIDIA, so there is no demand side to list into. The Mac-in-the-cloud providers (MacStadium, SKORPPIO) own their own hardware and rent it for Xcode/CI; they are not marketplaces you can join.

Running the machine at full tilt around the clock — an outcome you have no mechanism to achieve — would still lose about $1,200 a year.

How the recommendation shifts with the actual sale price

The one input worth verifying

The $18,000 figure is an estimate off asking prices. Here is the same analysis run at a pessimistic and an optimistic clearing price, holding everything else constant at 8%.

The recommendation survives all three. Even if the machine clears at only $12,000 — well below the lowest current ask — the freed capital still buys nearly twice the tokens the machine can generate running flat out. The sale price changes how good the trade is, not whether it is good.

The one combination where holding wins

Stack every pessimistic assumption at once — a $12,000 sale, a 4% return, and genuine 24/7 utilization — and the capital yields $420/yr, or 483M tokens, just under the machine's 536M ceiling. That is the only corner of the analysis where keeping it comes out ahead.

It also requires renting the machine out around the clock, which no existing marketplace lets you do. Absent that, the scenario collapses back to ~44.7M tokens a year and the argument is not close.

Put another way

At 8%, $1,320/year is $110/month — enough to fully fund a $100/month Claude Max subscription, in perpetuity, without touching the principal. That subscription gives you frontier-model quality the local machine cannot produce at any speed.

Or, in frontier tokens: 52.8M Claude Opus 5 output tokens per year — about 4.4M a month.

On the LEAPS bar

The 4–12% band is still a generic opportunity cost, not a pick. The SPCX row is a labeled hypothetical: two estimated $100 Jan 2029 calls, marked to a $420 Dec 2028 input. A 4× bar is what that input produces when you lever it; it is not a finding about the Mac Studio, and it is not a forecast of SPCX.

The two decisions are still separable. The machine loses money at 2h/day and at 24/7 utilization, before any comparator is introduced. Whether the unlocked cash should buy LEAPS, sit in cash, or fund the business is a different question — and one worth taking to someone licensed rather than settling from this page.

The honest case for holding

Three arguments that cut the other way. They are real; they are just probably not worth $16,500.

1 · You may not be able to buy it back

If 512 GB of unified memory becomes something you genuinely need in 12–18 months, the option may not exist. DRAM supply is the binding constraint and nobody knows when it eases. That is real option value — weigh it against the cash sitting idle in a startup that likely has better uses for it.

2 · The dollar value was never the point

Privacy on sensitive data, working offline, no rate limits, unlimited experimentation without metering anxiety. None of these appear in the tables above and all of them are legitimate reasons to own local compute. The question is whether they need 512 GB — and for AlpacApps, they don't.

3 · Carrying cost may be negative right now

While the shortage persists, the machine may be appreciating faster than it depreciates — meaning it currently earns more sitting on the desk than it produces in tokens. That is an argument for timing, not for holding indefinitely. It also cuts the other way: appreciation is exactly why selling now beats selling later.

What 512 GB is actually for

Nothing AlpacApps does needs it. Bulk vision over work_photos, permit OCR, the CAD render pipeline — all 30B-class workloads, several of which already run on Alpuca's 24 GB. The only thing 512 GB unlocks is frontier-scale open models like R1 671B, and those lose to cloud Claude on both quality and economics.

You'd be holding an $18,000 asset to run models you've already concluded you'd rather run in the cloud.

Recommendation

  1. Verify the real number first. Check eBay sold listings and r/hardwareswap. Every figure on this page rests on an $18,000 estimate derived from asking prices, and asks on thin markets are unreliable. Per the sensitivity table above, the recommendation holds anywhere from $12K to $25K — so this is worth an hour of checking, not a week of it.
  2. Sell into the shortage. Scarcity pricing is when you get paid. Waiting for an M5 Ultra to ship means selling a two-generation-old machine into a normalized memory market.
  3. Buy no replacement. Alpuca already handles every local workload the business actually runs. The $1,499 mini is a marginal upgrade over hardware on the desk.
  4. Keep the split you already documented. LOCAL-AI-SETUP.md concluded local models are for privacy, offline work, and bulk classification — and cloud Claude for anything correctness-sensitive. Selling the Studio doesn't change that split. It funds it.
