💺 Rear-seat crash protection is the number that matters

Your kids will ride in the second row, in boosters, for years. Texas law requires a child safety seat until age 8 unless the child is 4'9" or taller. Crash tests have historically focused on the driver. IIHS's updated moderate-overlap test now puts a dummy in the back seat, and it's where the two cars separate:

🌡️ Austin heat is a real hazard for kids

Texas leads the nation in child hot-car deaths (NoHeatstroke.org). Every parent thinks it could never happen to them. The car can be the backstop:

  • Child Left Alone Detection: the 2026 Model Y's cabin radar senses a child left in the car, flashes the lights, sounds a tone and pushes an alert to your phone. It's on by default.
  • Cabin Overheat Protection keeps the cabin below 105°F for up to 12 hours after you walk away.
  • Camp Mode keeps the AC running indefinitely while you wait in the car with sleeping kids; pre-cool the car from your phone before pickup.
  • Sentry Mode records anyone who approaches the parked car.

BMW offers remote climate start through the My BMW app. We couldn't confirm a radar-based child-presence detector on the X5 or iX from BMW USA, so ask the dealer directly.

👪 Room to grow: the Model Y L

If grandparents, carpools or a third car seat are in the picture, the Model Y L (US launch July 2026, $61,990) adds a third row: heated, power-reclining second-row captain's chairs, a heated third row with child-seat anchors, and an 8-inch rear touchscreen. That's still about $10K less than the new X5, which dropped its third row for 2027.

Honest caveat: IIHS rates the Model Y's LATCH child-seat anchors “Acceptable” (the X5 gets “Good”) and its seat-belt reminders “Marginal”. At 6, your kids are in belt-positioned boosters that don't rely on LATCH, but expect installs to take a bit more effort.

🚘 What it is today

FSD (Supervised) v14.3 drives door to door: school drop-off, MoPac, I-35, parking lots. You keep your eyes on the road and take over when needed. It's $99/month, and you can turn it on and off; Tesla stopped selling it outright in February 2026. It runs best on cars with the AI4 computer, which every new Model Y has.

The value for parents: when one of the kids drops a tablet or starts a fight in the back seat, the car is still watching all eight cameras.

🏙️ Why Austin specifically

Austin is where Tesla's driverless future is happening first. Tesla's robotaxis have run here since June 2025, with no safety monitor inside since January 2026, across ~245 square miles including the airport. Cybercabs are built at Giga Texas and have given paid rides in Austin since September 2026. On its October 21, 2026 earnings call, Tesla said customer cars could first drive unsupervised around Q2 2027, inside robotaxi zones, and Austin is the biggest one.

Tesla's timelines have slipped before (as recently as April it said Q4 2026), so treat the date as a goal, not a promise. But if it lands, an Austin family is among the first to get a car that can drive the kids home while the parents are passengers too.

What BMW offers: Highway Assistant
  • Hands-free driving on divided highways, up to 85 mph on the iX (BMW says up to 80 mph on the new X5).
  • Changes lanes when you glance at the mirror. Part of an optional Driving Assistant Professional package (~$2,500 on the 2027 X5).
  • Highways only, eyes on the road at all times. No city streets, no school zones, no roadmap to driving unsupervised.
The other side. NHTSA has open investigations into FSD: an engineering analysis of 3.2M vehicles over cameras blinded by glare, fog or dust (9 incidents, 1 death), and a probe into red-light running and wrong-lane driving (80 complaints by December 2025). Austin robotaxis have 36 incident reports on file, mostly minor. FSD is supervised for a reason: it's a co-pilot, not a chauffeur, until regulators say otherwise.

🔮 The bigger question: what happens when cars drive themselves?

Historical depreciation assumes the future looks like the past. Self-driving may break that. Two forces to weigh for a car you'll sell around 2031:

The bull case for Tesla
If owners' cars go unsupervised in 2027–28, a used AI4 Model Y becomes a car that drives itself, maybe one that can earn money on Tesla's network. Buyers will pay for that. Meanwhile ARK Invest has long forecast that cheap robotaxi rides will pull down used values for ordinary cars: why pay for a gas SUV when autonomous rides cost a fraction of ownership? A 2026 gas X5 has no path to autonomy.
The bear case (be clear-eyed)
Used Tesla prices fell hard in 2025 (Model Y down ~16% year over year) as Tesla cut new-car prices and the $7,500 federal credit ended. ARK's forecast is a forecast, not data. And if robotaxis get cheap enough, they could pressure all used-car values, Teslas included. Tesla's FSD timelines have slipped for years.
The bottom line on resale
On today's numbers, the Model Y already loses fewer dollars than the X5 and far fewer than the iX. Self-driving adds an asymmetric bet: if autonomy arrives roughly on schedule, the Tesla's value gets a floor the BMW can't have; if it doesn't, you still come out ahead in dollars. A 2026 gas SUV is betting that the next five years look like the last fifty.

Even with FSD subscribed for all five years, the Model Y comes in ~$10,900 under the X5. The 6-seat Model Y L still costs about $10,000 less than the 5-seat X5.
  • It's beautiful and it drives beautifully. The X5's interior materials, ride and steering feel are a class above the Model Y. If driving is your joy, that's a real thing.
  • Better percentage resale, particularly in years 1–3 (29% vs 44% lost).
  • Easier car-seat installs: IIHS rates X5 LATCH “Good” vs. “Acceptable” for the Model Y.
  • No charging to think about on road trips to Dallas, Houston or the Hill Country, though the Supercharger network along I-35 and I-10 is dense.
  • A brand you don't have to explain, if that matters to you.
The trade
You'd be paying roughly $17,000 more over five years for a nicer interior and driving feel, while accepting a 4-star NHTSA rating, nearly double the rollover risk, a weaker rear-seat rating, and a car with no path to driving itself. If the X5 is a want, the Model Y is the need, for these two passengers especially.
The verdict
Get the Model Y. Put FSD on it. Take the BMW for a weekend rental.
Five stars and the lowest rollover risk, the best rear-seat crash rating, radar that won't let a child be forgotten in the Texas heat, about $17K back in your pocket, and a seat at the front of the self-driving shift in the city where it's happening first. Need room for more? The Model Y L still beats the X5 on price and gives you a third row.
