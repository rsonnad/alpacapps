01 · Set up

Before the first note

Make four choices now and the rest of the page will read literally. Every fret number below is counted from the capo.

3

Capo

Clamp it just behind the 3rd fret. Treat the capo as the new nut.

E

Tuning

Use standard tuning: E A D G B e, low to high.

RH

Picking hand

Use thumb, index, middle. The ring finger can relax.

52

Starting speed

Begin at 48–52 BPM. Evenness matters more than speed.

02 · Hand map

Give every finger one job

The motion becomes much easier when your picking hand stops making decisions. Park each finger over its home string and let the thumb travel.

The rule: P plays the bass and D string. Index always takes G. Middle always takes B. Keep the hand quiet.

Tab number = fret. A zero means open. An x means do not play. Read time from left to right.

03 · The engine

Seven notes. Eight spaces.

The final empty space is not a mistake—it is what lets the chord breathe and your hand reset before the next bass note.

C · G/B · Am7: bass is string 5G · D/F♯ · Em: swap both 5s for string 6

Say it before you play it

P · I · P · M
P · I · P · —
  1. Mute the strings with your left hand.
  2. Loop the motion until all seven attacks are equally spaced.
  3. Add one C chord. Let every note keep ringing.
  4. Only then add the chord changes.

The last “&” is silent. Hear the previous D-string note continue through it.

04 · Shapes

Build the chord family

The first three shapes do most of the work. The remaining shapes turn the corner into the pre-chorus and chorus. Numbers inside the dots are fretting fingers: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 pinky.

↻
The C → G/B → Am7 pivot

Keep the index finger near B-string fret 1. For G/B, the pinky reaches B-string fret 3; then lift it and drop the middle finger to D-string fret 2 for Am7. Small movements, no flying fingers.

05 · Main loop

When the bass moves to string 6

For G and D/F♯, use the same seven-note hand motion but replace both string-5 bass notes with string 6. These two examples make the switch literal.

07 · Practice

Climb in five small steps

Check off a step only when you can repeat it three times without stopping. Your progress stays on this device.

50 BPM
52 BPM
60 BPM
72–80

The pattern keeps collapsing

Return to muted strings and say the finger names aloud. The left hand is not the problem yet.

The B string sounds harsh

Pluck toward your palm, not away from the guitar. Use less middle-finger force.

Chord changes create gaps

Move immediately after the D-string note. The silent eighth slot is your built-in transition window.

The thumb loses its bass

Practice only strings 5–4–5–4, then 6–4–6–4. Add I and M after the pendulum is automatic.

Everything is too tense

Let the picking wrist float. A tiny motion at the last finger joint is enough.

It sounds correct but stiff

Make beats 1 and 3 slightly warmer—not louder. Let the other notes fall around the bass.
