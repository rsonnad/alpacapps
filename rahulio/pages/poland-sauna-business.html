Understand the Immigration Landscape

⚠️ Critical Change for Americans

The US-Poland bilateral agreement that previously allowed Americans to stay beyond 90 days ended in October 2025. US citizens are now limited to the standard 90 days within any 180-day period under Schengen rules. Plan your residence permit application early.

🛂 Your Immigration Path

As a US citizen, you can enter Poland visa-free for 90 days. During that window, you must:

  1. Register your company (Week 1-2)
  2. Apply for a Temporary Residence Permit (TRP) for business activity (before Day 60)
  3. The TRP application itself gives you legal stay while it's being processed
💡 ETIAS Note

Starting mid-2026, Americans will need an ETIAS travel authorization (similar to ESTA for the US) before entering the Schengen Area. It's a simple online form (~€7), valid for 3 years. Apply at travel-europe.europa.eu/etias.

Choose Your Business Entity

The overwhelming choice for foreign investors in Poland is the Sp. z o.o. (Spolka z ograniczona odpowiedzialnoscia) — a limited liability company equivalent to an LLC/GmbH.

✅ Sp. z o.o. (Recommended)

95% of foreign investors choose this. Limited liability, can have a single shareholder, no Polish residency required for directors. Minimum capital PLN 5,000 (~$1,250 USD). Supports the Temporary Residence Permit application.

❌ JDG (Sole Proprietorship)

Only available to EU/EEA citizens, permanent residents, or holders of specific permits. Not an option for Americans on initial entry. Also offers no liability protection for a construction project.

🏗️ Why Sp. z o.o. for a Sauna Business
  • Liability shield — essential when you're building a physical facility with construction risk
  • Property ownership — the company can own real estate directly (avoids personal foreign-buyer restrictions on land)
  • Credibility — Polish banks, contractors, and municipalities take Sp. z o.o. seriously
  • Tax-efficient — 9% CIT rate for small taxpayers (revenue under EUR 2M)

Register Your Sp. z o.o.

There are two registration paths. For speed, the S24 online system is ideal — but it requires a qualified electronic signature or a trusted profile (profil zaufany), which as a non-resident American you likely won't have initially.

⚡ S24 Online System

1-3 business days. Electronic registration through the Ministry of Justice portal. Uses standardized articles of association (model template). Requires a qualified electronic signature or Polish trusted profile. Capital paid to company bank account within 7 days of registration.

📝 Notarial Registration

2-4 weeks. Traditional path through a Polish notary. Fully customizable articles of association (better for complex ownership structures). Capital must be paid before registration. Notary handles all KRS (court register) filing. Recommended for your first time.

📋 What You'll Need
  • US passport (apostilled copy may be requested)
  • Proof of address — US or Polish temporary address
  • Company name — check availability at ekrs.ms.gov.pl
  • Registered office address in Poland (can use virtual office initially)
  • Articles of association (notary will prepare)
  • Initial capital — PLN 5,000 minimum deposited
  • PKD code — 96.04.Z (Physical well-being activities) is your primary code

🔢 Registration Outputs

After registration, your company receives:

  • KRS number — National Court Register number (your official company ID)
  • NIP — Tax identification number
  • REGON — Statistical number from GUS (Central Statistical Office)

These are generated automatically upon KRS registration. Total registration fees: PLN ~600 (KRS fee + Monitor Sadowy publication).

5

Apply for Temporary Residence Permit

⏰ Timing is Everything

Apply no later than 45 days before your 90-day visa-free period expires. The act of filing the application extends your legal stay until a decision is made. Processing takes 4-12 months depending on voivodeship office backlog. Warsaw is slowest; smaller cities can be faster.

📄 TRP for Business Activity — Requirements

Under Article 142 of the Polish Foreigners Act, you must demonstrate at least one of:

  1. Revenue requirement: Company generates income at least equal to 12x the average monthly salary in your voivodeship for the preceding year (roughly PLN 7,000-8,000/month in Warsaw, less elsewhere)
  2. Employment requirement: Company employs at least 2 Polish citizens full-time
  3. Investment proof: Demonstrate significant capital investment that will generate the above (this is your strongest path with a $1M investment)

📋 Documents for TRP Application

  • Completed application form (available at your voivodeship office)
  • 4 recent passport photos (3.5cm x 4.5cm, Polish standard)
  • Valid passport + copy of all pages
  • Proof of health insurance valid in Poland
  • Proof of stable income or sufficient funds
  • Proof of accommodation (rental contract or property deed)
  • KRS extract for your Sp. z o.o.
  • Company financial statements / business plan
  • Tax declarations (PIT/CIT if available)
  • Employment contracts if claiming the employment path
  • Stamp fee: PLN 340 (~$85)
✅ Your $1M Advantage

With a $1M+ investment plan for a sauna facility, you have a very strong case. Submit a detailed business plan showing projected employment (construction crews → permanent staff), projected revenue, and the property development plan. The voivodeship office looks favorably on significant investment that creates Polish jobs. Consider hiring your first two Polish employees early (even part-time) to satisfy the employment criterion upfront.

6

Get Your PESEL & Register Address

🔢 PESEL Number

The PESEL is Poland's universal identification number (like an SSN). You need it for:

  • Tax returns and dealings with tax office
  • Opening personal bank accounts
  • Healthcare (NFZ) registration
  • Property purchase (notarial deeds)
  • Signing contracts

You receive a PESEL automatically when you register your address (zameldowanie/meldunek) at your local gmina (municipality) office, or you can apply separately at any gmina office. Bring your passport and rental agreement.

🏠 Address Registration (Zameldowanie)

Register your address at the local Urzad Gminy (municipality office) within 30 days of finding accommodation. You need:

  • Passport
  • Rental contract or property ownership document
  • Landlord's consent (if renting — they may need to sign the form)

This is free and usually processed the same day.

7

Acquire Property for Your Sauna

🚨 Foreign Property Purchase Rules

As a non-EU citizen, purchasing land, standalone buildings, or properties with land requires a permit from the Ministry of Interior and Administration (MSWiA). Apartments (in multi-unit buildings) can be purchased freely without a permit. Since a sauna facility requires land, you will need this permit.

🏗️ The MSWiA Permit Process

  1. Identify your property — sign a preliminary purchase agreement (umowa przedwstepna) with a condition that the sale is subject to obtaining the MSWiA permit
  2. Gather documents:
    • Proof of ties to Poland (registered company, address registration, TRP application)
    • Preliminary purchase agreement
    • Land registry excerpt (ksiega wieczysta)
    • Local zoning plan excerpt or zoning decision
    • Property survey/map
    • Your financial means proof
  3. Submit application to MSWiA with PLN 1,570 stamp fee
  4. Wait 4-12 weeks for the decision (sometimes longer)
  5. Complete purchase at notary within 2 years of permit issuance
💡 The Company-Ownership Workaround

If your Sp. z o.o. is controlled by a foreigner (you, as sole or majority shareholder), the company also needs an MSWiA permit to buy land. However, a company where Polish partners hold majority shares can purchase land freely. If you have a trusted Polish business partner, this structure can bypass the permit requirement entirely. Alternatively, buying shares in an existing Polish company that already owns the land is another common approach.

🗺️ Property Considerations for a Sauna Complex

  • Zoning (MPZP): Check the local zoning plan at the gmina. You need land zoned for commercial/service use (uslugi) or mixed-use. Agricultural land requires a costly and slow "de-agriculturation" (odrolnienie) process.
  • Utilities: Water supply is critical for saunas. Verify water main connection capacity, sewage, and electrical capacity (saunas are power-hungry: 15-50 kW per commercial sauna).
  • Access road: Must have legal road access. Check the land register (ksiega wieczysta) for easements.
  • Environmental: Check for Natura 2000 protected areas, water protection zones, or flood plains.
  • Size: For a $1M build-out, expect 1,000-3,000 m² of land minimum for the facility, parking, and outdoor areas.
🔑 Alternative: Long-Term Lease

If the MSWiA permit process is too slow for your timeline, consider a 30-year commercial lease (dzierzawa). This doesn't require a permit, gives you enough tenure to justify building, and the lease can be registered in the land register for protection. Many foreign investors start with a lease and negotiate a purchase option for later.

Construction Planning & Building Permits

📐 The Polish Construction Process

  1. Hire an architect (architekt) — must be licensed in Poland (registered with Izba Architektow). They will design the facility and manage the permit application.
  2. Obtain a zoning decision (decyzja o warunkach zabudowy) if no local zoning plan exists for your parcel.
  3. Commission a geological survey (badania geologiczne) — required for the building permit application.
  4. Prepare the building design (projekt budowlany) — must comply with Polish building codes (Prawo Budowlane).
  5. Apply for building permit (pozwolenie na budowe) at the local Starostwo Powiatowe (county office). Processing: 65 days legally, often 3-4 months in practice.
  6. Appoint a site manager (kierownik budowy) — required by law. Must be a licensed Polish construction engineer.
  7. Build — construction log (dziennik budowy) must be maintained.
  8. Obtain occupancy permit (pozwolenie na uzytkowanie) from the building inspectorate (PINB) before opening.
🧖 Sauna-Specific Requirements
  • Sanitary inspection (Sanepid): Mandatory approval from the State Sanitary Inspection for any facility with pools, saunas, or steam rooms. Covers water quality, ventilation, hygiene standards.
  • Fire safety (Strazy Pozarna): Fire department inspection required before opening. Saunas have specific fire safety requirements (emergency exits, fire-resistant materials, sprinkler systems for areas >1000 m²).
  • Water/waste: If you have pools or plunge pools, you need a water treatment system approved by Sanepid. Wastewater from saunas may need special handling.
  • Electrical: Commercial sauna heaters (400V, 3-phase) require a licensed electrician and separate electrical inspection.
  • Accessibility: Polish building code requires disabled access for public buildings.

💡 No Special "Sauna License"

Poland does not have a specific sauna license. Saunas fall under general wellness/recreation services (PKD 96.04.Z). The main regulatory hurdles are Sanepid (health inspection) and fire safety. If you add a pool or spa, you'll also need to comply with the Minister of Health's regulation on swimming pool water quality.

Hire Your Team

👥 Staffing a Sauna Facility

For a mid-to-large sauna complex, expect to hire:

  • Sauna masters (saunamistrz) — trained in aufguss ceremonies, essential for premium positioning. Poland has a growing sauna master community.
  • Reception / front desk — at least one bilingual (Polish/English) staff member
  • Cleaning / maintenance — critical for Sanepid compliance
  • Lifeguard (ratownik) — legally required if you have any pools deeper than 1.2m
  • Bookkeeper (ksiegowa) — required for Sp. z o.o. tax compliance (outsource to biuro rachunkowe initially)
  • Massage therapists — if offering spa services, they need relevant qualifications
💰 Employment Costs in Poland (2026)
  • Minimum wage: ~PLN 4,700/month gross (~$1,175)
  • Average sauna staff salary: PLN 5,000-7,000/month gross (~$1,250-$1,750)
  • Employer's social insurance (ZUS): ~20% on top of gross salary
  • Total employer cost: roughly 1.2x the gross salary
  • Remember: Hiring 2 Polish citizens full-time satisfies your TRP requirement

🇺🇸 US Tax Obligations

As a US citizen, you're taxed on worldwide income regardless of where you live. The US-Poland Tax Treaty prevents double taxation. You'll need to:

  • File US tax returns annually (Form 1040 + Form 2555 for foreign earned income exclusion)
  • Report foreign bank accounts (FBAR / FinCEN 114) if combined balances exceed $10,000
  • File FATCA Form 8938 if foreign assets exceed $200K ($400K joint)
  • Report ownership in foreign corporation (Form 5471 for Sp. z o.o.)
  • Hire a US tax accountant experienced with expat taxation — this is not optional

💡 Budget Reality Check

$1M is a solid budget for a mid-size sauna complex in Poland outside of Warsaw. In Warsaw, you'll need $1.5-2M. The best value is in cities like Wroclaw, Poznan, or Lublin where land is 3-5x cheaper than Warsaw but still has strong demand. Lake/mountain regions (Masuria, Podhale) are ideal for resort-style saunas but need seasonal demand planning.

🧖 Learn from the Best

Poland has a thriving sauna culture. Visit these established facilities for inspiration:

  • Termy Chocholow (Podhale) — mountain thermal baths, massive scale

  • Termy Uniejow — geothermal spa, smaller city success story

Join the Polskie Towarzystwo Saunowe (Polish Sauna Society) for networking and industry knowledge.

⚖️ Hire a Polish Law Firm First

This is the single most important investment you'll make. A good law firm experienced with foreign investors will:

  • Handle company registration end-to-end
  • Manage the TRP application
  • Guide the property purchase and MSWiA permit
  • Review all construction contracts
  • Navigate Sanepid and fire inspections

Budget PLN 20,000-40,000 ($5K-$10K) for the first year of legal support. Recommended: firms in your target city with English-speaking partners and track records with US clients.

🌐 Learn Polish

While many Poles speak English (especially in cities), your daily interactions with contractors, government offices, and staff will be vastly smoother in Polish. Commit to at least B1 level before opening. Resources: Italki for tutors, Babbel for structured courses, Krok po Kroku textbook series (the gold standard for Polish learners).
