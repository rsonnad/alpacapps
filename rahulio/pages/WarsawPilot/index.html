Warsaw Pilot — Business Plan

The Idea

Spaces That Think

Today's "smart" spaces are dumb. A smart home is a collection of apps — one for lights, one for thermostats, one for music. You're the orchestrator. You manually set scenes, write automations, and troubleshoot when things break. The intelligence is in your head, not in the room.

Three Pillars builds physical spaces where the AI is the operator. Not an app you control — an intelligence that controls the environment. It sees who's in the room, understands what's happening, and shapes the space in real time: adjusting lighting, temperature, sound, scent, and access without anyone touching a switch.

The space becomes a living system. It responds to people the way a great host would — anticipating needs, creating atmosphere, and adapting moment to moment.

Architecture

Three Layers

Every Three Pillars space is built on three layers that work together:

Physical Layer
The hardware. Smart lighting (tunable color temperature + intensity per zone), climate control (HVAC + radiant panels + air quality), directional speakers, motorized shading, electronic access control, scent diffusion, displays. Everything is addressable — the AI can reach every actuator in the space.
Intelligence Layer
The AI operator. Sensor fusion (occupancy, light levels, temperature, humidity, noise, CO2). Contextual awareness (time of day, calendar events, who's present, what's happening). Decision engine that translates context into physical actions. Learns from outcomes over time.
Experience Layer
What people feel. The AI doesn't expose its controls — it produces experiences. "Focused work" isn't a button; it's what happens when the AI dims the lights, cools the air 1°, silences the speakers, and locks the door. The output is a feeling, not a setting.
AI Agency

What the AI Controls

The AI has direct control over every environmental system in the space. Not suggestions — actual control.

💡
Lighting
Per-zone color temperature, intensity, and rhythm. Circadian shifts. Accent lighting for mood. Follows natural daylight patterns.
🌡
Climate
Temperature, humidity, airflow, CO2 monitoring. Zone-by-zone control. Preheats and precools based on schedule and occupancy.
🔊
Sound
Directional speakers per zone. Background music, white noise, silence. Volume and genre shift with context. Announcement capability.
🚪
Access
Electronic locks, presence detection, guest access codes. AI grants and revokes access based on schedule, identity, and context.
🪟
Shading
Motorized blinds and curtains. Responds to sun position, glare, privacy needs, and time of day. Balances natural light with thermal load.
🌿
Atmosphere
Scent diffusion, air purification, plant irrigation. Subtle environmental cues that shape how a room feels without anyone noticing why.

Sensing

The AI's decisions are driven by what it senses:

  • Occupancy & presence: Who is in which zone, how many people, movement patterns
  • Environmental: Temperature, humidity, CO2, light levels, noise levels per zone
  • Calendar & schedule: What events are happening, who's expected, what's coming next
  • Time & season: Time of day, day of week, sunrise/sunset, season
  • Behavioral patterns: How people use the space over time — when they arrive, where they sit, what they prefer
  • External: Weather, outdoor temperature, air quality, pollen count
In Practice

A Saturday at Three Pillars

Here's what the AI does during a community gathering — no human touches a control.

Scenario • Community Gathering • Saturday 3:00 PM
40 members arriving over 20 minutes for afternoon tea
2:00 PM
Pre-arrival. AI starts the space. HVAC targets 21.5°C. Lights shift to warm 2700K at 60%. Background music begins — ambient, low volume. Blinds adjust for afternoon sun angle. Front door unlocks. autonomous
3:00 PM
Arrival wave. Presence sensors detect people entering. AI increases ventilation as CO2 begins to rise. Lights brighten slightly to match social energy. Music volume adjusts to stay just below conversation level. reactive
3:20 PM
Full room. 38 people detected. Temperature drops 0.5°C to compensate for body heat. AI routes audio to directional speakers — different ambient tracks for the main hall vs. breakout nooks. Scent system activates a light tea-adjacent aroma. predictive
4:00 PM
Community moment. Calendar event triggers. Main hall lights brighten to 80%. Breakout nook lights dim to discourage side conversations. Speakers in main hall switch to voice-optimized EQ. Background music fades to silence. event-driven
4:15 PM
Breakout pairs. Members disperse. AI detects occupancy shifting to nooks. Each occupied nook gets warm lighting, slight temperature bump, and its own audio zone. Unoccupied nooks stay dim. zone-aware
5:15 PM
Wind-down. AI begins the departure sequence. Lights shift cooler. Music transitions to something signaling closure. Temperature edges down. As occupancy drops below 10, some zones go to standby. autonomous
5:45 PM
Close. Last person exits. AI locks doors, sets overnight climate (16°C), kills audio, drops lights to security-only. Logs the session. autonomous

This is one scenario. The same space runs differently for focused coworking, a dinner party, a workshop, or an empty afternoon. The AI adapts the environment to whatever is happening.

Design Principles

How We Build

01
AI Operates, Humans Override
The AI runs the space by default. Humans can always override — but they rarely need to. The best compliment: "I didn't notice the technology."
02
Feelings, Not Settings
Nobody says "set lights to 3200K at 70%." They say "this room feels calm." We design for feelings and let the AI translate to hardware.
03
Every Surface Wired
If the AI can't reach it, it can't control it. Every light, vent, speaker, lock, and shade is on the network. No dead zones.
04
Learn From Every Session
Every event is training data. The AI gets better at predicting what the space needs. Month 1 is good. Month 12 is invisible.
05
Physical Materials Matter
Technology disappears into the walls. The visible layer is warm, natural, and human — wood, ceramics, plants, textiles. The space should feel like a home run by a thoughtful person, not a tech demo.
The Pilot

Why Warsaw

  • Construction costs: Fit-out and renovation costs are 40–60% lower than Western Europe or the US, while craftsmanship quality is exceptional
  • Tech talent: Poland is a top-5 European tech hub. Finding engineers who can wire IoT, build AI pipelines, and integrate hardware is realistic
  • Real estate: Commercial spaces in central Warsaw are available at prices that allow a pilot without raising $10M first
  • Community hunger: Young professionals who moved to Warsaw from smaller cities are actively seeking belonging and third spaces
  • Cultural fit: Polish kameralno (intimate gathering) culture values exactly the kind of warm, curated environment we're building
The space is the product. The AI is the operator. The humans just show up and feel it.

Pilot Space

The first Three Pillars space is a single venue in central Warsaw — approximately 200–300 m²:

  • Main hall: 80–100 m². 6 communal tables, open plan, high ceilings. This is where group events happen.
  • Nooks: 8–12 small breakout areas around the perimeter. 2–4 person seating. Semi-private.
  • Kitchen/service: Tea and light food preparation. Not a restaurant — curated beverages and snacks.
  • Tech room: Server rack, network infrastructure, AI compute. The brain of the space, hidden from view.

Phase 2: The Platform

Once the AI operator is proven in one space, the real business is licensing it to other venues:

  • Co-working spaces that want AI-managed environments without building the tech themselves
  • Hospitality — hotels, restaurants, and wellness centers that want rooms that adapt to guests
  • Corporate offices that want meeting rooms, lounges, and workspaces that self-optimize
  • Residential — high-end apartments and homes where the AI runs the house

The Warsaw space is both a business and a showroom. Every visitor experiences what the AI can do.

Next Steps

Path to Opening

  1. Find the space — scout 5–8 commercial venues in Śródmieście, Mokotów, or Praga. 200–300 m², high ceilings, good bones.
  2. Design the fit-out — architect + IoT engineer collaborate on a plan where every surface is wired and every zone is independently controllable.
  3. Build the AI operator — sensor integration, decision engine, hardware control APIs. Start simple: lighting + climate + audio. Add capabilities over time.
  4. Renovate & install — 8–12 week build-out. Physical construction and hardware installation happen in parallel with software development.
  5. Recruit founding members — 30 people for the soft launch. Referral-based. People who will give real feedback.
  6. Open & iterate — run weekly events. Let the AI learn. Fix what breaks. The first month is about data collection. By month 3, the space should feel alive.
