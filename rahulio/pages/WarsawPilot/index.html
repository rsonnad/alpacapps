The Problem

Social Networks Failed

You have 1,000 followers and zero real friends. Instagram knows your face but not your name. LinkedIn has your résumé but has never seen you laugh. Bumble BFF matches you with strangers for coffee that goes nowhere. Every social network built in the last 20 years has optimized for engagement on a screen — not for actual human relationships.

The result: a loneliness epidemic. Half of adults report having fewer close friends than they did a decade ago. People are more connected digitally and more isolated physically than at any point in history.

The problem isn't that people don't want connection. It's that no one has built the infrastructure for it to happen reliably in the real world.

The Insight

Real Relationships Need Real Places

Think about your best friendships. They didn't form through a swipe or a follow. They formed through shared physical experience — sweating together, eating together, struggling together, laughing in the same room. Getting your hands dirty in a garden. Feeding animals at dawn. Sitting around a fire after a cold plunge. Proximity plus shared intensity plus repeated exposure. That's the formula. It's always been the formula.

No app can replicate sitting across from someone in a sauna. No algorithm can substitute for harvesting tomatoes side by side. No feed can replace breaking bread together every Saturday with food you grew. The medium matters. And the medium for real relationships isn't a screen or even a building — it's land. A real place with soil, weather, seasons, animals, and sky.

What if you built a social network where the "feed" was a garden, the "algorithm" was an AI that knew exactly who you should plant next to, and the "engagement metric" was whether people actually became friends?

The best social network ever built was a piece of land where people gathered. We're scaling it with AI.
What We Build

A Social Network That Runs in Physical Space

Three Pillars is the first social network where the platform is a real place: a warehouse next to a field. The warehouse houses the saunas, studios, communal tables, and crowdsourced kitchen. The field holds the gardens, animals, and open-air gathering spaces. Indoor and outdoor, built environment and natural environment, working together. Not an app. Not a coworking space. A place with walls and sky where the AI builds your social graph by putting you alongside the right people, in the right context, over and over until the relationships are real.

Both halves are the infrastructure. Inside: saunas you sweat in together, studios you move in together, tables you eat at together. Outside: gardens you tend together, animals you care for together, sky overhead. The experience flows between indoor and outdoor — it integrates you into the environment rather than sealing you off from it. And the product isn't the warehouse or the field. The product is the connections that form across both.

Social Network vs. Social Club

This distinction matters:

A Social Club

Opens the door and lets you figure it out. The same people cluster in the same corners. Newcomers feel lost. Connections are random. The space is nice but the network is accidental.

An IRL Social Network

An AI knows every member's goals, skills, personality, and history. It assigns your seat. It puts you in the sauna with someone who can change your career. It ensures every visit adds a new edge to your social graph. Nothing is random.

The Engine

The AI Social Graph

This is the core technology. Every digital social network runs on a social graph — a map of who knows who and how they're connected. Facebook's graph is likes and friend requests. LinkedIn's is endorsements and job titles. Ours is built from something no digital platform has: real-world interaction data.

Member Profiles
What you tell us. Goals (health, education, social, spiritual), skills, interests, what you want to learn, what you can teach, gardening experience, comfort with animals, dietary preferences, physical comfort zones. Updated continuously. The raw inputs to the matching engine.
Interaction Graph
What actually happened. Who sat together, who gardened together, who did morning animal duties together, who exchanged contacts, who came back the next week because of someone they met, which pairings generated the most energy and follow-ups. Digital networks guess at connection strength. We measure it — including in the garden and the coop, not just at the table.
Relationship Outcomes
Did it work? Post-visit feedback: "Who helped you today?" "Who do you want to see again?" "Who did you help?" Connection requests (double opt-in). The AI learns which kinds of pairings produce lasting relationships and which fizzle. It gets better every week.

The Infrastructure

Where Connections Happen

Every social network needs infrastructure. Facebook's is servers and data centers. Ours is a warehouse and a field. Inside: saunas, studios, tables, kitchen. Outside: gardens, animals, open air. Each element is designed not for its standalone function but for the type of social connection it produces — and the flow between indoor and outdoor keeps people integrated into the environment, not sealed off from it.

🌱 Gardens
Connection type: Working the land together. Herbs, vegetables, flowers, fruit trees. Nothing builds a relationship like kneeling in dirt side by side. Gardening is slow, tactile, and meditative — it strips away performance and leaves two people just being human together. The harvest feeds Saturday's communal meal. And the AI is watching — garden cameras track plant health, soil conditions, and growth progress, then guide volunteer gardeners on what to do next: "The tomatoes in bed 3 need staking. The basil is ready to harvest. Bed 7 needs weeding." Members don't need gardening expertise. The AI provides it.
Network function: Grounding layer. AI vision monitors the garden, assigns plots, pairs members for tasks, and coaches them through the work. Anyone can garden here — the AI is the master gardener.
🐔 Animals
Connection type: Shared care. Chickens, goats, bees. Caring for animals together is one of the oldest forms of human bonding. Fresh eggs for the kitchen, honey for the table, and a reason to show up on Tuesday morning that has nothing to do with networking. Animals don't care about your job title.
Network function: Responsibility layer. The AI coordinates care schedules, pairs experienced and new members for morning duties. Caring for something alive together creates a different kind of trust.
🍽 Socializing Tables
Connection type: Structured introductions. Long communal tables inside the warehouse where 64 members sit in AI-assigned groups of 8. In warm weather, tables extend outside into the field. Table displays show prompts and context. This is the "feed" — curated encounters designed to produce real relationships, not content.
Network function: Primary matching surface. AI assigns seats, crafts prompts, tracks which pairings generate follow-ups.
🧖 Saunas
Connection type: Unguarded conversation. Heat strips away pretense. No phones, no agenda. People talk honestly in saunas — about real things, not networking small talk. Some of the deepest connections form here, not at the tables.
Network function: Deepening layer. The AI routes complementary members into the same sessions.
❄️ Cold Plunges
Connection type: Shared intensity. Nothing bonds strangers faster than surviving something hard together. You cheer each other through 3 minutes of ice water. A powerful social equalizer — the CEO and the barista are equally uncomfortable.
Network function: Trust accelerator. Shared adversity compresses months of relationship-building into minutes.
♨️ Hot Tubs
Connection type: Intimate conversation. Small groups, warm water, no time pressure. People talk for hours here. The most naturally social facility — no structure needed, the environment does the work.
Network function: Relationship maintenance. Where new connections become real friendships through extended time together.
💃 Dance Studio
Connection type: Nonverbal bonding. Moving together builds connection faster than talking. Dance classes, ecstatic dance nights, movement workshops. You learn someone's personality through how they move before they ever speak.
Network function: Social ice-breaker. Physical activity lowers social barriers that conversation alone can't.
💪 Fitness Studio
Connection type: Shared struggle. Group workouts, partner exercises, training sessions. Sweating together builds trust. You spot someone on a heavy set and you're bonded in a way no DM can replicate.
Network function: Trust layer. Physical cooperation creates implicit trust that accelerates social connection.
🧘 Yoga Studio
Connection type: Shared vulnerability. Yoga, breathwork, meditation. Being still and open in a room of people creates a quiet intimacy. The counterbalance to the sauna's heat and the tables' social energy.
Network function: Depth layer. Members who practice together develop a different kind of bond — less verbal, more trust.
🍜 Food & Drink
Connection type: Generosity. Crowdsourced kitchen — members and vendors cook and share using common infrastructure. Herbs from the garden, eggs from the chickens, honey from the bees. When you serve someone a meal made from things you grew together, that's not a transaction. That's a bond.
Network function: Giving layer. The kitchen connects the garden to the table. The AI coordinates harvest, vendor rotation, and menu planning.
A garden + the right person = a relationship you'll have for 20 years. No app on earth can do that.
The Network Effect

The Flywheel

Every social network lives or dies by its network effect. Ours is physical, which makes it more powerful — and harder to copy.

1
Better Matches
More members = more data = the AI gets smarter at pairing people. Week 1, it guesses. Week 20, it knows exactly who you need to meet.
2
Deeper Relationships
Better matches = people click faster = connections deepen. Members stop coming for the sauna. They come because their people are there.
3
Organic Referrals
Deep relationships = members invite people they love. "You have to come Saturday." The network grows through word of mouth, not ads.
4
Network Value Compounds
More great people = richer mix = every visit gets better. The 100th member makes the network exponentially more valuable than the 10th.
5
The Moat: You Can't Copy the Graph
Someone can build a nicer sauna. They can't replicate the social graph. The relationships, the AI's accumulated knowledge of who clicks with whom, the culture of giving — that's years of compounding data and trust. It's the same moat Facebook has, but built on something real.
The Culture

A Network That Runs on Giving

Every social network has a currency. Facebook's is likes. Twitter's is retweets. Ours is giving. The most valued members aren't the ones with the most connections — they're the ones who give the most to others.

Here's the insight: when a place provides genuine abundance — land that produces food, animals that produce eggs and honey, a body heated by the sauna, cooled by the plunge, moved by dance, fed by the community kitchen, surrounded by real relationships and open sky — scarcity dissolves. And when you're not in scarcity, the single greatest dopamine hit is giving something amazing to someone else.

How Giving Works in the Network

🍜 Feed the Network
Cook for the community using the crowdsourced kitchen — with herbs from the garden, eggs from the chickens, honey from the bees. Your grandmother's recipe made with ingredients you grew together. Giving is as simple as making something with your hands from the land.
🌱 Grow for the Network
Tend the gardens. Feed the animals. Harvest what's ready. The most ancient form of giving: you put something in the ground, care for it, and hand the result to someone else. The garden is the purest expression of the giving culture.
🎓 Teach the Network
Dance classes, fitness sessions, beekeeping, composting, workshops, skill-shares — all taught by members. The AI identifies gaps: "6 members want to learn salsa. You've been dancing for years. Would you lead a class?"
🧖 Welcome the Network
Leading a sauna session for newcomers. Showing someone how to collect eggs. Guiding a breathwork circle. Experienced members become the network's connective tissue by welcoming new ones into the rhythms of the land.
🤝 Givers Get the Best Network
The AI rewards contribution with connection. High contributors get the best table assignments, first access to interesting new members, and deeper matches. The currency of giving buys the richest social experience.
In a world of sustainable abundance, your greatest high is giving something incredible to someone else. The network creates the abundance. The AI makes the giving effortless.

Why Now

Four things are converging:

  • Digital social networks are failing. Instagram, TikTok, and Twitter are optimizing for engagement, not relationships. Users are burning out. Trust is collapsing. People are disconnected from each other and from the physical world — from soil, seasons, and animals. The world is ready for a social network built on land, not screens.

  • Poland is the right launchpad. 40–60% of Western build costs. Top-5 European tech talent. Strong sauna culture, vibrant social scene, and deep community hunger among young professionals priced out of London and Berlin.
Every social network in history has been digital. The next great one will be built on land. And it will be the one where people actually become friends.
