What We Do

Physical Spaces, Infused with AI

Three Pillars is a technology company that builds and operates physical spaces designed for human growth. Each space is a real venue — socializing tables, saunas, cold plunges, hot tubs, studios, and a crowdsourced food market — where an AI layer operates the environment, connects people, and helps members reach their goals.

Think of it as a country club for your soul, run by AI.

The AI doesn't live on a screen. It lives in the space. It controls the sauna temperature, manages the cold plunge cycles, runs the lighting and music in the studios, schedules the vendor kitchen, assigns seating at the weekly gathering of 64 members, and learns over time what each person needs to grow. The technology is invisible. The experience is a place that takes care of you.

The Technology Thesis

Most AI companies are building software. We're building the real-world interface — physical spaces where AI has agency over the environment and can directly impact human wellbeing. The insight is that AI's most transformative application isn't chatbots or image generation. It's operating the physical world around people in ways that make them healthier, wiser, more connected, and more grounded.

The Stack

Three Layers

Every Three Pillars space runs on the same architecture:

Physical Layer
The venue. Socializing tables, saunas, cold plunges, hot tubs, dance studio, fitness studio, yoga studio, crowdsourced kitchen. Plus the hardware: smart lighting, climate controls, directional audio, displays, sensors (occupancy, temperature, humidity, CO2, water quality), electronic access, POS systems. Everything addressable by the AI.
AI Layer
The operator. Sensor fusion, contextual awareness, decision engine. The AI sees who's present, what's scheduled, what each member's goals are, and what happened last week. It translates all of this into physical actions: adjusting the sauna, managing vendor rotation, assigning tables, cueing the hosts, shifting the lights. It learns from outcomes and gets better every week.
Experience Layer
What people feel. Members don't interact with the AI — they interact with the space, and the space is the AI. The sauna is at your preferred temperature. The studio class you love is on the schedule. The person seated across from you at Saturday's gathering has exactly the skill you need. Nobody touches a control. It just works.
The Space

The Core Components

Every Three Pillars location is built from the same set of core components — each one AI-operated, designed for social connection and physical wellbeing.

🍽 Socializing Tables
The heart of the space. Long communal tables where 64 people gather weekly in AI-assigned groups of 8. Table displays show conversation prompts and member context. The tables are where giving happens — knowledge, introductions, support, laughter.
AI controls: Seating assignments, table prompts, displays, transition cues, lighting per table zone
🧖 Saunas
Finnish sauna and infrared rooms. Multiple temperature zones. Social by design — conversation flows naturally when people are relaxed and unplugged. Some of the best connections happen here, not at the tables.
AI controls: Temperature cycles, session timing, ventilation, lighting mood, occupancy limits
❄️ Cold Plunges
Cold water immersion pools for contrast therapy and mental resilience. The shared intensity of cold plunge creates instant bonding — you cheer each other through it. A powerful social equalizer.
AI controls: Water temperature, filtration cycles, session timing, usage tracking
♨️ Hot Tubs
Warm water soaking for recovery and conversation. The most naturally social facility in the space — people talk for hours here. Small groups, intimate setting, no agenda.
AI controls: Water temperature, jets, lighting, ambient audio, scheduling
💃 Dance Studio
Sprung floor, mirrors, sound system. For dance classes, movement workshops, group fitness, ecstatic dance nights. Movement is medicine, and doing it together builds bonds faster than any conversation.
AI controls: Audio system, lighting (shifts with music energy), class scheduling, instructor coordination
💪 Fitness Studio
Functional fitness equipment, free weights, resistance training. Not a globo gym — curated for the community. Group workouts and personal training sessions. Sweating together builds trust.
AI controls: Lighting/music intensity, class coordination, equipment booking, workout suggestions
🧘 Yoga Studio
Warm, quiet space for yoga, breathwork, meditation, and stillness practices. Natural materials, soft light, no shoes. The counterbalance to the sauna's intensity and the tables' social energy.
AI controls: Ambient lighting, temperature (warm for yoga, cool for breathwork), sound, do-not-disturb
🍜 Food & Drink
Crowdsourced food and drink vending with shared infrastructure. Members and local vendors can sell their food, drinks, baked goods, and specialties using the space's kitchen, prep areas, and point-of-sale system. The AI manages scheduling, inventory, and vendor rotation. Not a restaurant — a food marketplace run by the community.
AI controls: Vendor scheduling, kitchen booking, inventory tracking, menu displays, payment processing

The Crowdsourced Kitchen

The food and drink model deserves emphasis. Three Pillars doesn't operate a restaurant. It provides the infrastructure — commercial kitchen, prep stations, serving areas, refrigeration, point-of-sale — and members or local vendors bring the food. A member who bakes sourdough can sell it on Saturdays. A local pierogi maker can set up a station during gatherings. Someone who roasts their own coffee can pour for the community.

The AI manages the logistics: who's vending when, what's available, kitchen scheduling, health compliance tracking, and payments. The result is a constantly rotating, community-driven food scene where the best thing you can give is something you made with your hands.

The Culture

A Place That Runs on Giving

Here's the insight: when a space provides genuine abundance — a body heated by the sauna, cooled by the plunge, moved by dance, fed by the community kitchen, surrounded by real relationships — scarcity dissolves. And when you're not in scarcity, the single greatest dopamine hit is giving something amazing to someone else. Watching their eyes light up. Knowing you made their day.

Three Pillars is built on this premise. The space takes care of your needs so thoroughly that your natural impulse shifts from "What can I get?" to "What can I give?" The AI doesn't force generosity. It creates the conditions where generosity is the most rewarding thing you can do — and then it makes giving effortless.

How the Space Encourages Giving

🍜 The Kitchen Is Community-Fed
The food comes from members and local vendors, not a hired chef. When you bring your grandmother's pierogi recipe and serve it on Saturday, that's a gift. The crowdsourced kitchen makes giving as simple as cooking something you love.
🎓 Members Teach Members
The dance classes, fitness sessions, yoga, and workshops are taught by members sharing what they know. The AI identifies who has skills others need: "6 members want to learn salsa. You've been dancing for years. Would you lead a class?"
🧖 Shared Rituals
Leading a sauna session. Guiding a breathwork circle. Hosting a cold plunge challenge. The facilities become vehicles for giving when experienced members welcome newcomers and share what they've learned about their own practice.
🤝 AI Rewards Givers
The AI tracks contribution — who teaches, who feeds the community, who mentors, who shows up for others. High contributors get the best matches at Saturday gatherings. The most interesting new members flow toward generous people. Giving is rewarded with connection.

The Contribution Score

The AI maintains an invisible contribution score for every member. It's never displayed as a number — no leaderboards, no gamification. But it shapes the experience:

  • High contributors get priority breakout pairings, first access to new members, and are chosen for workshop teaching opportunities
  • Steady contributors get the full experience — this is most members
  • Low contributors get gentle nudges from the AI: "Marta is leading a beginner yoga class Wednesday. Want to help her set up?" It offers specific, low-friction ways to give.
  • Persistent takers get a quiet conversation from a host. Not a punishment — an invitation to shift. If it doesn't change, they're counseled out to protect the culture.

Built Into the Gathering

The Saturday event itself is structured around giving:

  • Table prompts are outward-focused: "What's something you could help someone at this table with?" not "What do you need?"
  • The Give Wall: A physical board near the entrance where members post offers — "I can teach you to make sourdough," "I can help with your business plan," "Free headshots this week"
  • Connection requests are about helping: When the AI suggests someone to meet, it frames it as mutual: "You can help her with X. She can help you with Y."
  • The communal meal: Eating food that members and vendors made with their own hands is the most primal form of giving. Every Saturday, generosity is on the table — literally.
In a world of sustainable abundance, your greatest high is giving something incredible to someone else. The space creates the abundance. The AI makes the giving effortless.

Feedback Loop

The Real Metric: Did Your Giving Land?

Most communities measure what members receive. We measure what members give — and whether the people on the receiving end valued it. That's the evaluation function. Not "did you get value?" but "are people loving what you're giving them?"

This flips the entire incentive structure. When you know the AI is tracking how your contributions land — and rewarding you with better connections, richer experiences, and deeper belonging when they do — your natural impulse is to give better, not take more.

After Every Visit

The questions center on what you gave and what others gave you:

  • Who helped you today? — tap names. "Marta showed me a breathing technique that changed everything." This is how givers get recognized.
  • Did you give something? — Taught a class / Fed people / Helped someone / Shared knowledge / Led a session / Listened / Other
  • Best moment: "What's one thing someone did for you that made your day?" — free text, optional but powerful
  • Connection: Tap to request contact from someone (double opt-in)

The Giving Score

The AI builds a picture of each member's giving impact over time:

  • How often are you named by others? When people say "Tomek helped me today," Tomek's giving score rises. This is the primary signal — it comes from the recipients, not the giver.
  • What kind of giving? Teaching, mentoring, physical labor, emotional support, sharing skills. The AI learns your giving style.
  • Is it landing? If you teach a workshop and people rate it highly, that's a strong signal. If you share advice at the table and three people request your contact, that's a strong signal. The AI measures impact, not intention.
  • Reciprocity balance: The AI watches whether you're mostly giving, mostly receiving, or in a healthy exchange. All three can be fine — new members receive more, experienced members give more. But persistent imbalance gets attention.

Monthly Reflection

Once a month, a deeper check-in — but notice the framing:

  • "What's the best thing you gave this month?" — free text. Forces reflection on contribution.
  • "Who at Three Pillars has made the biggest difference for you?" — name someone. This person gets a quiet recognition from the AI.
  • "What do you want to give next month?" — teach a class / cook for the community / lead a sauna ritual / mentor someone / other. The AI matches this with community needs.
  • Growth check: Health / Education / Social / Spiritual — 1–5 each. Still tracked, but secondary to the giving metrics.
  • Referral: "Would you invite someone you love?" — the ultimate signal that the giving culture is working.

Why Now

The Moment

Three things are converging:

  • AI is ready for the physical world. Sensor costs are down 90% in a decade. LLMs can reason about context. Edge compute can run decision engines locally. The technology to operate a physical space autonomously exists today.
  • People are starving for real places. Post-pandemic, the hunger for physical community, nature, and embodied experience is at an all-time high. Digital everything has left people disconnected from their bodies, from the land, and from each other.
  • Poland is the right launchpad. Exceptional construction quality at 40–60% of Western costs. Top-5 European tech talent. Strong sauna culture, vibrant dance and fitness scene, serious food culture, and deep community hunger among young professionals.
Every AI company is building for screens. We're building for sweat, movement, food, and the people who gather around them.
