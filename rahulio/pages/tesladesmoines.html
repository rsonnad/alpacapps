🩺 The uncomfortable truth about age and crashes

Here's what the research actually says — and it's not what most people assume. Older drivers don't crash dramatically more often than middle-aged drivers. The reason fatality rates climb after 70 is fragility: the same crash that a 45-year-old walks away from can be fatal for a 76-year-old, mostly due to chest injuries and medical complications. IIHS found fragility accounts for about 77% of the elevated death rate for drivers ages 75–79.

What does that mean for John & Colleen? It means the single most powerful thing you can do for your safety is put a car around you that (1) does everything possible to avoid the crash, and (2) protects your body extraordinarily well if one happens. That is precisely what the Model Y is built to do.

4× & 3×
Drivers 75 and older are about 4 times as likely to die in a side-impact crash and about 3 times as likely in a frontal crash as middle-aged drivers in the same collision. The crash is often identical — the outcome isn't. (IIHS, Fatality Facts 2024)

📊 Where the danger actually is

For the oldest drivers, the highest-risk situations are multiple-vehicle intersection crashes — they account for 46% of fatal crashes for drivers 85+, versus about 24% for middle-aged drivers. Left turns, cross traffic, that car you didn't see coming. These are exactly the moments where a car that watches all directions and brakes on its own changes the outcome.

Avoiding the crash (intersections, cross-traffic) Huge — where automatic braking earns its keep
Driving more miles / driving worse Smaller factor — retirees often drive less, not worse

🚚 The trap to avoid: the “retirement car”

IIHS has a pointed warning for exactly this decision. Drivers 70+ disproportionately hang on to older, smaller, lighter cars — what they call “retirement vehicles,” often 16+ years old and missing modern safety tech. The data is stark: vehicles without electronic stability control had 37% higher fatality odds for drivers 70+, and those without head-protecting side airbags doubled the risk. Only about 1 in 4 older drivers' cars even had automatic emergency braking.

IIHS estimates that if older drivers simply drove vehicles with the same safety profile as middle-aged drivers, it would save roughly 90 lives a year. The lesson isn't “buy something small and easy to park.” It's the opposite: buy the safest, most technology-forward car you can — especially now.

The bottom line
At 76, safety isn't about driving more carefully — you already do. It's about the machine wrapped around you when something goes wrong. Choosing a car with the best crash protection and the best crash-avoidance technology is the most effective health decision on this list, and it's a one-time purchase decision. The Model Y is the strongest answer money can buy.

🧩 Why the lots look empty — and why that's not a problem

Iowa law restricts car manufacturers from selling directly to the public, so Tesla can't run a traditional sales dealership in the state the way Ford or Toyota do. That's the real reason you're seeing “few Teslas in Iowa.” But here's the good news: you were never limited to what's sitting on an Iowa lot. Tesla's whole model is built around ordering remotely and delivering to you, and that works fine for Iowans. There are three clean paths:

1️⃣ See and drive one locally — Urbandale

Tesla operates a Gallery and Service Center at 2601 104th St, Urbandale, IA 50322, right in the Des Moines metro. The gallery is open Tuesday–Saturday, you can look over the Model Y in person, and you can book a demo drive. This is almost certainly the “dealership” you're standing in. Use it to sit in the car and drive it — then order whichever way makes sense.

2️⃣ Buy a new one — order online, no haggling

A new Model Y is ordered on Tesla's website at a fixed, published price — no negotiating, no back-and-forth, no add-ons. You pick the color and trim, and Tesla arranges delivery. Leasing is done the same way, right in the order screen. For many people this is a relief: the price is the price, and it's the same for everyone.

3️⃣ Buy a used one — and have it shipped in

Used Teslas can be sold by anyone, so these are widely available. Two great options: Tesla's own used inventory (search the whole country and have the car delivered to Urbandale — this single fact erases the “few in Iowa” problem), and local used-car dealers — for example, BMW of Des Moines recently listed a 2024 Long Range with about 7,500 miles. CarGurus, Carvana, cars.com, and Edmunds all show what's available near you right now.

🔧 Service & charging, handled

The Urbandale Service Center covers the metro, and Tesla's mobile service can come to your home for many repairs — no trip needed. For charging, Tesla will help arrange a simple home charger install in your garage; you plug in at night and never think about it. Road trips use the Supercharger network, which is the most reliable fast-charging in the country.

“Few Teslas in Iowa” is a myth once you order remotely
Between the Urbandale gallery, online ordering for new cars, and nationwide used inventory delivered to your door, John & Colleen have more Model Y choice than they'd have at any single dealership — not less. The thin local lots are an illusion created by an old law, not a real limit on what you can get.

🔋 Why a used Tesla with some miles is still “barely broken in”

An electric motor has roughly 20 moving parts versus about 2,000 in a gas engine — no oil to degrade, no timing belt to snap, no transmission to fail. Tesla batteries are warrantied to 120,000 miles and routinely last 200,000–300,000+ miles with most of their range intact. A Model Y with 40,000–50,000 miles is in its early youth.

That's why the higher-mileage used cars below are often the best value: a real discount, with very little mechanical risk. You're not inheriting someone's worn-out engine — there isn't one.
