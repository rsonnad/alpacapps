🇵🇱 Treaty Status

Poland & the E-2 Visa

Poland is an E-2 treaty country under the US-Poland Business and Economic Relations Treaty (1990). Polish citizens are fully eligible for E-2 Treaty Investor visas.

💼

Invest & Direct

Invest a substantial amount in a US business and actively direct its operations. Must own ≥50%.

💰

Capital at Risk

Money must be irrevocably committed — spent on equipment, leases, payroll. Not sitting idle in a bank.

📈

Not Marginal

The business must generate (or project to within 5 years) income well beyond a minimal living for the investor.

✅ Key Advantage: Spouse Work Authorization E-2 dependent spouses receive automatic work authorization (EAD) — they can work for any US employer in any field. Children under 21 can attend school.
⚠️ Important Limitation The E-2 does not directly lead to a green card. However, it is renewable indefinitely and many holders transition to EB-5, EB-1C, or other immigrant categories while maintaining E-2 status.
📋 Checklist

What USCIS Requires

The E-2 has no fixed dollar minimum — it uses a proportionality test. Here are the six pillars of qualification.

1
Treaty Nationality

Must be a citizen of Poland (treaty country since 1990). Dual citizenship works if Polish passport is used.

2
Substantial Investment

No fixed amount — uses the “proportionality test” (inverted sliding scale). Lower-cost businesses need a higher percentage invested. Typically $100K+ for strong applications.

3
Capital Irrevocably Committed

Funds must be spent or contractually committed — lease deposits, equipment purchases, payroll funded. Escrow counts, idle bank balances do not.

4
Lawful Source of Funds

Full paper trail required: bank statements (6–12 months), tax returns, property sales, inheritance docs. Crypto gains need exchange records and conversion documentation.

5
Active Direction & Control

Must own ≥50% and hold a managerial role. Cannot be a passive investor — must be actively directing day-to-day operations.

6
Non-Marginal Enterprise

Must create jobs for US workers and generate income beyond a minimal living. A 5-year business plan projecting growth is essential for new businesses.

📅 Timeline

Realistic Timeline: Start to Visa

From initial planning to having the E-2 visa in hand. Total: approximately 4–8 months depending on business complexity and consular scheduling.

Months 1–2: Foundation
Entity Formation & Attorney Engagement
Form LLC/Corp in Texas. Hire E-2 immigration attorney ($8K–$15K). Begin source-of-funds documentation (bank statements, tax returns, crypto exchange records). Engage CPA for financial trail.
Months 2–3: Investment Execution
Commit Capital & Build the Business
Sign commercial lease. Purchase equipment / aircraft. Hire initial employees. Begin FAA Part 141 process (if flight school). Every expenditure creates paper trail proving “at risk” investment.
Months 3–4: Application Prep
Business Plan & Filing Package
Professional 5-year business plan ($2K–$5K). Financial projections with industry benchmarks. Organizational chart. Complete DS-160 + DS-156E. Compile supporting evidence binder.
Months 4–5: Filing
Submit Application
Option A: Consular Processing at US Embassy Warsaw (2–8 weeks to interview). Option B: USCIS Change of Status (if currently in US on valid visa — ~5 months standard, 15 days premium processing for $2,805).
Months 5–6: Interview
Consular Interview (Warsaw)
In-person interview at US Embassy in Warsaw. Bring all original financial documents, business evidence, and personal identification. Most decisions are made same-day.
Month 6–7: Visa Issued
E-2 Visa In Hand 🎉
Visa stamp issued in passport (12-month validity for Polish citizens, multiple entry). Enter the US — CBP grants 2-year period of stay. Begin operating the business. Renewable indefinitely.
⏰ If Already in the US on a Valid Visa You can file a Change of Status (I-129) with USCIS instead of traveling to Warsaw. Standard processing: ~5 months. Premium processing: 15 business days ($2,805 extra). You can continue working on your current visa while the COS is pending.

💡 Pro Tip: Phased Approach Start with the business that gets approved fastest (franchise or property management). Once the E-2 is secured, use the stable visa status to build the flight school in parallel. This de-risks the entire process — you have legal status while the higher-investment venture ramps up.
💰 Using Crypto as Source of Funds USCIS accepts crypto gains as legitimate investment capital, but you need: (1) Full purchase history showing when you acquired crypto, (2) Exchange records from US-registered exchanges, (3) Conversion records to USD, (4) Bank statements showing USD deposit. Start this documentation NOW — it takes weeks to compile. Use a CPA experienced with crypto.
