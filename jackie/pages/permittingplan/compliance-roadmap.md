# Commercial Permit Compliance Roadmap
## 160 Still Forest Dr, Cedar Creek, TX 78612 — Lot 14-B, Block 6, Blue Bonnet Acres

**Document purpose.** This roadmap translates the 73-task `permit_tasks` checklist into a readable critical-path narrative for the owner. It explains *why* each phase exists, *how* the phases chain together, *where* approval can fail, and *which decisions only the owner can make*. The checklist tells you what to do; this document tells you what order to do it in and what to watch for.

---

## 1. Executive summary

**Classification.** The project is a **commercial Non-Single-Family Development** under Bastrop County rules, triggered by the presence of **three or more dwelling / lodging units** on a single lot. Because 160 Still Forest Dr is in **unincorporated Bastrop County** (Cedar Creek is a Census-Designated Place, not a city), there is no municipal zoning, no B3 Code, and no City of Bastrop homestead affidavit shortcut. The governing authorities are:

- **Bastrop County Development Services** (platting, site development, OSSF, stormwater, driveway, fees)
- **Applicable Emergency Services District (ESD)** (fire access, hydrant flow, Knox Box, IFC 2021 life-safety)
- **USFWS / LPHCP** (Houston toad habitat, impervious-cover mitigation)
- **Lost Pines Groundwater Conservation District** (well permit, groundwater availability)
- **Bastrop Central Appraisal District (BCAD)** (mixed-use reappraisal)
- **Blue Bonnet Acres POA** (private deed restrictions — contractual, not public law)

**The three hardest hurdles specific to this site.**

1. **The bathroom building already under construction without an approved IDP** (tracked as B26). This is legally a Class B misdemeanor under county development regs. The only viable path forward is *proactive disclosure at the pre-application meeting* and working a retroactive compliance plan into the IDP. Any attempt to hide it will be discovered at the engineer site visit (A8) and will compound the problem.
2. **Setback violations on existing structures** (tracked as C1). The survey shows the Big Trailer at ~1 ft from the west property line (needs 25 ft), two red containers at ~2 ft (need 25 ft), and the beige container at ~6 ft from the south line (needs 25 ft). These must be physically relocated before the Certificate of Compliance can issue. Relocation is a hard, expensive task that shapes the entire site plan.
3. **LPHCP / Houston toad habitat status** (A3 → B17 → B28 → B41). Bastrop County is core Houston toad country and the Lost Pines Habitat Conservation Plan is the single most unpredictable regulatory overlay in the county. Whether this parcel is in designated habitat, and whether cumulative impervious cover stays under 1 acre, determines whether you file a simple Declination of Coverage (B17) or must record an Implementing Agreement / Conservation Easement with the County Clerk (B41) — the latter adds $1,500–$3,000 in mitigation fees and a legally binding encumbrance on the land forever.

## 2. Sequence of operations (the critical path)

### Phase A — Pre-Development & Legal Foundation
*"Can we even do this, and who do we have to talk to first?"*

The purpose of Phase A is to lock down everything the county needs to see *before* they will accept a formal development application. Skipping any Phase A task guarantees a 10-day completeness-review rejection.

**Parallel tasks (can all happen the same week):**
- A1 Determine ESD coverage (needed to address A7 email and B9 letter)
- A2 Floodplain status — ✅ already DONE (Zone X, not in floodplain)
- A3 Houston Toad habitat check — **unblocks B17 or B28/B41 branch**
- A10 Legal-lot verification — confirms Lot 14-B is a legal parcel under LGC Ch. 232 (no new platting needed if yes)
- A11 Blue Bonnet Acres deed restriction review — POA can sue to halt construction even after county approves
- A12 SB 2440 groundwater availability statement (licensed engineer/geoscientist)
- A15 Property tax-current certificate from BCAD — required with initial app
- A16 MyGovernmentOnline portal registration — administrative blocker for all uploads

**Sequential tasks (must happen in order):**
- A4 Draft preliminary site plan → A6 Development description → A7 Email county for pre-dev meeting → A5 Attend Pre-Development Meeting
- A8 Engineer site visit + A9 Septic specialist site visit — both *before* any PE work begins, so the engineer and designer can see actual conditions

**The Pre-Development Meeting (A5) is the single most important event in Phase A.** It is where you (a) proactively disclose the bathroom building (B26), (b) get a binding read on LPHCP treatment, (c) confirm which roads are county vs state (B33), and (d) surface any surprise requirements before you spend money on engineering. Do not show up without the draft IDP sketch (B32), the development description (A6), and the Houston toad habitat lookup (A3) in hand.

### Phase B — Civil, Environmental & Infrastructure Documents
*"Everything stamped, sealed, and attached to the application."*

Phase B is the paperwork mountain. It is where the licensed Texas PE, the licensed OSSF designer, and the RPLS (registered professional land surveyor) earn their fees. The 10-day county completeness review happens on the *Phase B submission*, not on pre-app conversations.

**Engineering track (Texas PE):**
- B4 PE-signed/sealed site plan
- B5 Drainage study + B35 Engineering drainage study (sealed, §VIII) + B36 Erosion & sediment control + B37 Grading plan with FFE + B38 TR-55 detention analysis
- B14 Internal roads plan (supports C11 fire apparatus access specs)
- B22 Parking plan + B23 Signage plan + B24 Trash & waste collection plan

**Survey track (RPLS):**
- B3 Copy of survey — ✅ HAVE (2021, Jason Ward RPLS #5811)
- B27 Survey update to pin *proposed* trailer/container locations
- (Later at C13: Final as-built survey after construction)

**OSSF track (licensed designer):**
- B11 OSSF permit application + B12 septic plan/specs + B13 soil evaluation report (or B34 NRCS update) + B30 aerobic maintenance contract + Clerk affidavit + B39 OSSF permit issuance

**Environmental track:**
- B17 LPHCP Declination (if NOT in habitat) — single form, free
- OR B28 Cumulative impervious-cover calc → B41 Implementing Agreement or Conservation Easement recorded with County Clerk (if IN habitat and >0.5 ac impact)

**Utility availability letters:**
- B6 Water (Lost Pines GCD groundwater certification for private well) + B20 Groundwater district cert
- B7 Electric (Bluebonnet Electric Cooperative)
- B8 Gas (confirm at pre-dev; may not apply)
- B9 ESD compliance letter — blocks nothing but required in package
- B21 Well water quality testing (bacteria, nitrates, commercial-use parameters)

**Administrative:**
- B1 Development application form filled
- B2 Proof of ownership — ✅ HAVE (Revocable Trust deed)
- B15 Driveway permit (county road, $50)
- B16 Operator information
- B18 9-1-1 addresses assigned for each unit (free, county-assigned)
- B19 Owner's agent certificate (if anyone other than trustee submits)
- B25 Application fee payment — app not processed without it
- B26 Bathroom building retroactive compliance path
- B29 Knox Box spec + ESD coordination
- B31 Contractor GL insurance proof ($200k+)
- B32 Sketch draft IDP (for pre-app meeting, then superseded by B4)
- B33 Confirm Still Forest Dr jurisdiction — ✅ CR 329 is county, not TxDOT
- B40 Floodplain permit — ✅ not required (Zone X)

**The HB 3167 "shot clock."** Once Phase B is submitted, the county has 10 business days to run a completeness check and 30 working days for technical review. If Correction Items are issued, the clock *pauses* until the engineer replies. This is why submitting an incomplete package is worse than waiting — a rejected completeness check wastes 10 days; a well-prepared package can close in 45 days.

### Phase C — Building, Fire & Construction
*"Moving dirt and fixing what's already wrong."*

Phase C is the physical work. It cannot begin until the Infrastructure Development Plan (IDP) is approved from Phase B. In Phase C, the site is brought into actual compliance with the approved drawings.

1. **C1 Relocate structures** — Big Trailer, red containers, beige container moved out of setback. This is often the biggest physical cost item. Some owners choose to *remove* rather than relocate.
2. **C2 Road improvements** — internal roads brought to county spec (one-way 10 ft, two-way 20 ft, 9 in base compacted to 6 in, all-weather). Ties directly to C11 fire apparatus engineering specs.
3. **C3 Fire suppression tank installation** — 2,500-gallon non-metallic, 2.5 in NST coupling, adjacent to road, labeled non-potable, vented. (C12 covers the flow certification or alternate plan if this changes.)
4. **C4 Development entrance sign + C5 Per-unit 9-1-1 address signs** — low-dollar but required.
5. **C8 Water tap/connection + C9 Electric service extension + C10 Septic installation** — coordinate with Aqua WSC, Bluebonnet, and the licensed OSSF installer.
6. **C11 Fire apparatus access engineered specs** — internal roads must support 84,000 lb apparatus, 25 ft inside / 50 ft outside turning radius, 20 ft clear width, 6 in compacted base. This is an IFC 2021 Chapter 5 requirement enforced through the ESD letter.
7. **C12 Fire water supply flow certification or alternate plan** — tank draft OR hydrant flow test showing 250 gpm for 2 hours sustained.
8. **C13 Final as-built survey** — after all construction is physically complete, RPLS pins actual locations to verify they match the approved IDP. Without this, C6 and C7 cannot proceed.
9. **C6 Request county final inspection** — written notice to Development Services. County inspector must respond within the windows set by county order.
10. **C7 Certificate of Compliance issued** — this is the moment utilities can legally connect for commercial service and the permit is closed out.

### Phase D — Operations & Ongoing Compliance
*"You're open for business. Here's what you have to keep doing."*

Phase D tasks begin the day the Certificate of Compliance issues and never end.

- D1 HOT determination — if *any* tenant stays under 30 days, you must register with LocalGov and remit State 6% + Bastrop County 7%. If all stays are 30+ days, exempt but must document this.
- D2 Chapter 92 compliance package — smoke alarms, security devices, repair-and-deduct disclosure, security deposit policy, habitability standards.
- D3 BCAD mixed-use reappraisal coordination — preserves homestead on the owner-occupied portion while commercial improvements get reappraised separately.
- D4 Annual ESD/Fire Marshal inspection readiness — extinguishers every 75 ft, clear exits, alarm/sprinkler records.
- D5 3-year permit validity tracking — construction must be complete within 3 years of IDP issuance.

## 3. Cost envelope

These are rough buckets, not quotes. Use them for planning, not budgeting.

| Phase | Low | High | Biggest variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Pre-Dev | $500 | $2,500 | SB 2440 engineer statement ($500–$2,000) |
| B — Civil / Env / Infra | $15,000 | $40,000 | Civil PE package ($8–25k), OSSF design ($1,200 + aerobic adds $2–4k) |
| C — Construction | $30,000 | $120,000+ | Structure relocations (C1) and road improvements (C2) dominate |
| D — Operations (annual) | $500 | $2,500 | Aerobic maintenance contract, HOT remittance, annual ESD inspection prep |
| **Total, one-time** | **~$45,500** | **~$160,000+** | |

**Sensitivity notes.**
- If LPHCP impact >0.5 ac: **+$1,500**. If >1 ac: **+$3,000** (or recorded Conservation Easement + ongoing management).
- If existing OSSF (Jet J-500, 300 GPD) cannot handle the added rental load, new commercial OSSF can run **$8,000–$20,000** including installation.
- Structure *removal* is typically far cheaper than structure *relocation* — consider this at C1.
- Contractor GL insurance is *not* a cost to you; it's a requirement on whoever builds.

## 5. Decision points requiring owner input

These are places where the path branches and the owner must choose.

1. **Number of units.** 3+ triggers commercial classification. Going from 2 to 3 is a much bigger regulatory step than going from 3 to 5. Confirm final unit count before Phase B.
2. **Structures: relocate or remove?** (C1) Relocating a trailer or container is expensive and often damages the structure. Removing frees up site plan flexibility and may reduce impervious cover (helping B28 LPHCP math).
3. **LPHCP path:** Declination (B17) vs Implementing Agreement (B41-IA) vs Conservation Easement (B41-CE). CE waives fees on 4+ ac tracts but imposes active habitat management forever.
4. **OSSF: keep existing or install new?** If the licensed designer (A9, B12, B13) concludes the Jet J-500 cannot handle the commercial load, is it cheaper to upgrade to aerobic, install a new system, or reduce unit count to stay under existing capacity?
5. **Long-term vs short-term rentals.** D1 HOT obligations only trigger if any stay is under 30 days. Owner must commit to a business model and document it. Mixing is legally possible but administratively painful.
6. **Bathroom building disposition.** Complete it under a retroactive IDP, or remove it entirely? Removal is the cleanest legal path; completion is the cheapest economic path if the county accepts disclosure.
7. **Contractor selection.** Owner-build on homestead portion only is allowed; everything commercial *must* be built by a licensed contractor carrying $200k+ GL insurance (B31).
