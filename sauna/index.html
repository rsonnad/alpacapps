The Sauna Itself

Structure

The cabin is a Redwood Outdoors Thermowood Garden 8-Person Sauna. The interior is spacious, with an average height of about 8 feet sloping up to 10 feet at the peak. Thermowood is heat-treated timber, which makes for a more stable, rot-resistant shell — and, acoustically, a livelier room than a typical small sauna. That liveliness is wonderful for music and a real challenge for even coverage: the sloped ceiling means sound levels and frequency response shift noticeably between seating positions, which is exactly why the audio system is tuned the way it is.

Heat

  • Heat-up time: 20–40 minutes from cold. Turn it on before you need it.
  • Primary heater: conventional sauna stove, easy to switch on.
  • Infrared: there's also an infrared heater mounted on the ceiling, which you can run on its own or alongside the stove.
  • Lighting: the walk out to the sauna is lit by the sauna fence light, the stick pathway lights, and the sauna tree string — all controllable from the house lighting system.

Using It

  • Guests are welcome to help themselves to a sauna session — just allow for the heat-up time.
  • Shower first if you're sweaty, then hop between the sauna and the cold plunge. The plunge is always ready to go.
  • Close the cold plunge and swim spa covers when you're finished, and don't adjust the swim spa temperature.
  • A full bathroom with a hot-water shower is in the Outhouse, in the front yard; there's another shower behind the swim spa.
  • Clean up completely when you're done — that's the price of the free run of the place.
  • Please don't park in front of the sauna, no matter how tempting.
Want to book a session? Sauna and cold plunge evenings can be arranged — see Visiting for how to arrange one, or reach out through the Contact page and pick "Sauna/Spa Session."

The Sound System

A multi-channel immersive audio system built for sublime low end. Traditional speakers handle the music; bass shaker transducers bolted to the seating handle everything you feel rather than hear.

How to play music: use Spotify Connect or the Sonos S1 app while on the Black Rock City WiFi network, and select the Sauna room in Sonos. You can also AirPlay to the WiiM Pro receiver. The rack lives in the cabinet behind the sauna; volume and presets are adjusted on the miniDSP Flex 8.

Signal Flow

A single source feeds a miniDSP Flex 8 digital signal processor, which handles volume, routing, and all the DSP. From there the signal fans out to four independent paths — stereo mains, a dedicated subwoofer, and two chains of tactile transducers.

Design principle: the two Buttkicker amps are daisy-chained through the Line Out on the BKA-300-4 rather than fed by a splitter. No signal splitting, no integrity loss, and each amp keeps its own gain and filter settings.

Tuning

Each path is crossed over so the three layers stack instead of fighting each other:

PathFiltersLevel
Yamaha RX-V871Pure Direct, speakers "Small", crossover 80 Hz, EQ bypassedStart at −20 dB
LAB Gruppen E8:2HPF 25 Hz / LPF 100 Hz, −24 dB/oct, mono in, 4 ΩStart at 50%
BKA-300-4 (Auras)Low cut 25 Hz / high cut 80 Hz, −12 dB/oct, Line Out onStart at 40%
BKA-1000P (Buttkickers)Low cut 25 Hz / high cut 80 Hz, bridged, 1.4 V sensitivityStart at 50%

These are the amp-side settings the system was commissioned with. Once the Flex 8 tuning pass happens, the filtering collapses onto the Flex 8 and these downstream crossovers get flattened out — see The miniDSP Flex 8 below.

Wiring throughout is 12 AWG minimum. The Aurasound shakers run in true parallel for a 2 Ω load; the two Buttkickers run in series for 6 Ω. Phase alignment matters more than usual here — a transducer wired backwards cancels the very thing it exists to produce.

Living With Electronics in a Sauna

Heat and moisture are the enemy. Every amp, the Flex 8, and the Yamaha receiver live outside the sauna in a ventilated, lower-temperature equipment area. Only speaker wire runs inside the hot room. Heat kills electronics and trips thermal protection mid-session.
  • Cabling: marine-grade or outdoor-rated cable and connectors, with silicone boots on exposed jacks.
  • Runs: unbalanced runs stay under 10–15 ft; anything longer — especially the sub feed — goes balanced TRS-to-XLR.
  • Calibration: the UMIK-1 and REW handle room measurement. Reflective wood and a sloped ceiling produce big mid-bass peaks and dips; correct them gently, because heavy EQ sounds unnatural fast.
  • Rattles: tactile transducers will find every loose fastener in the benches. Re-check after any install.

The miniDSP Flex 8

The miniDSP Flex 8 sits between the source and the amplifiers, and it's installed and running as the front-end: input selection, volume, and all the crossovers, EQ, time alignment, and Dirac room correction in one box. With the Flex 8 in charge, each amp gets set to full-range with only its gain adjusted — no double or triple filtering, and consistent phase response across every output. Outputs 5–8 are spare, left for future expansion.

Still to come: the tuning pass. Installed is not the same as dialed in. Getting the payoff means someone on site with the UMIK-1 and REW, measuring the room, folding the crossovers off the downstream amps and onto the Flex 8, and applying correction — in a cedar box with a sloped ceiling that produces genuinely awkward mid-bass peaks. That realistically means bringing a sauna sound engineer out, which is a fair bit of overhead for an afternoon of measurement. Presumably, someday, an AI will just do this.
