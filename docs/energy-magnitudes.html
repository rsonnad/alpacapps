How big is energy?

A visual translation of Elon Musk's solar argument: human civilization is still small, today's grid is huge at human scale, and the sun is absurdly larger than both.

This poster uses Musk's figures as the narrative spine from the linked video segment. Where current public sources materially differ, the difference is called out in the footnotes. All power values are shown as approximate average power unless labeled as nameplate capacity.

The magnitude ladder

Layer Approx. average power What it means visually
1 GW 0.001 TW A major power plant or ambitious single space-solar project.
US electricity ~0.5 TW The number Musk is effectively using for "power the US" in the video segment.
US primary energy ~3 TW All direct fuel use plus electricity; much larger than electricity alone.

World primary energy ~19.6 TW Human civilization today: still a tiny pre-Type-I slice.

Footnotes and divergences

  1. Video basis: YouTube transcript beginning around 1:12:33; the specific solar-manufacturing comparison begins around 1:17:19. The QR tile in the hero points to that exact moment.
  2. US boundary difference: Musk's ~0.5 TW figure aligns with US electricity consumption/generation averaged over a year. US total primary energy is closer to 3 TW. See the EIA US energy facts and EIA electricity in the US.
  3. World, US, and China primary-energy fuel stacks use Energy Institute Statistical Review 2024-style primary-energy data converted from exajoules per year to average TW. A broad public overview is available from the Energy Institute Statistical Review.
  4. China solar manufacturing: Musk cites roughly 1.5 TW/year of solar panel production capacity. IEA public materials show China dominates global solar PV manufacturing, but published capacity numbers vary by manufacturing step, year, source, and whether the number is nameplate capacity, actual output, or planned capacity. See the IEA solar PV supply chains report.
  5. Solar land: the land estimate uses NREL-style utility-scale PV land intensity, rounded to about 30 acres per average MW-year after capacity-factor adjustment. See NREL's Land-Use Requirements for Solar Power Plants in the United States.
  6. Sun scale: total solar luminosity is around 3.8 x 10^26 W. Compared with current human primary energy near 2 x 10^13 W, the sun is not merely a billion times larger; total luminosity is roughly ten trillion times larger. Solar energy intercepted by Earth is a much smaller slice, but still thousands of times larger than human power use.
  7. Space solar: examples include Caltech's Space Solar Power Demonstrator, ESA's SOLARIS program, and recent commercial space-solar announcements such as Meta and Overview Energy's 1 GW plan. These are not yet comparable to terrestrial solar manufacturing at TW/year scale.
